## Section 1 — Intuisi: “Estimator yang Bagus” Itu Bagus Menurut Kriteria Apa?

Di [[4.5 Estimasi Parameter]], kita membangun estimator:

$$
\hat\theta=T(X_1,\ldots,X_n).
$$

Tetapi dua metode berbeda dapat menghasilkan dua estimator berbeda untuk parameter yang sama. Jadi pertanyaan berikutnya bukan lagi:

> “Bagaimana membentuk estimator?”

melainkan:

> **“Bagaimana menilai kualitas estimator?”**

Miller menekankan bahwa estimator adalah random variable, sehingga kualitasnya dinilai melalui **sampling distribution**.

Beberapa estimator mungkin:

- tepat secara rata-rata;
- memiliki variability kecil;
- makin dekat ke parameter saat $n$ membesar;
- merangkum seluruh information relevan di sample.

Tidak ada satu kata “best” tanpa criterion.

Mental model:

```text
Estimator T(X)
     ↓
sampling distribution
     ↓
evaluate:
mean / bias
variance
large-sample behavior
information retained
```

> [!DANGER] “Best Estimator” Trap
> Jangan menyatakan estimator A “lebih baik” dari estimator B tanpa menyebut criterion:
>
> - unbiased?
> - smaller variance?
> - lower MSE?
> - consistent?
> - sufficient?

# Section 2 — Unbiasedness

## 2.1 Definition

Miller mendefinisikan estimator $\hat\theta$ sebagai **unbiased estimator** dari $\theta$ jika dan hanya jika

$$
\boxed{
E_\theta[\hat\theta]=\theta
}
$$

untuk **semua possible values** dari $\theta$.

Jika

$$
E_\theta[\hat\theta]\ne\theta,
$$

estimator disebut biased.

### Bias Function

Bias untuk sample size $n$:

$$
\boxed{
b_n(\theta)
=
E_\theta[\hat\theta]-\theta
}.
$$

Unbiasedness berarti

$$
b_n(\theta)=0
$$

untuk seluruh parameter space.

> [!IMPORTANT] “For All $\theta$” Matters
> Jika estimator tepat hanya pada satu parameter value tertentu, estimator itu **tidak** menjadi unbiased secara umum.

---

## 2.2 Fundamental Example — Sample Proportion

Jika

$$
X\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,\theta),
$$

sample proportion adalah

$$
\hat\theta=\frac{X}{n}.
$$

Karena

$$
E[X]=n\theta,
$$

maka

$$
E\left[\frac{X}{n}\right]
=
\frac1nE[X]
=
\theta.
$$

Jadi

$$
\boxed{
\frac Xn
\text{ adalah unbiased estimator dari }\theta
}.
$$

---

## 2.3 Sample Mean sebagai Unbiased Estimator dari Population Mean

Jika

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n
$$

random sample dari population dengan mean $\mu$, maka

$$
\bar X=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nX_i.
$$

Karena linearity of expectation,

$$
E[\bar X]
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nE[X_i]
=
\frac1n(n\mu)
=
\mu.
$$

Sehingga

$$
\boxed{
\bar X \text{ unbiased untuk }\mu
}.
$$

---

## 2.4 Mengapa Sample Variance Membagi dengan $n-1$?

Miller menjelaskan bahwa

$$
S^2
=
\frac1{n-1}
\sum_{i=1}^n
(X_i-\bar X)^2
$$

dipilih karena, untuk random sample dari infinite population dengan finite variance $\sigma^2$,

$$
\boxed{
E[S^2]=\sigma^2
}.
$$

### Derivation

Gunakan identity

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2
=
\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mu)^2
-
n(\bar X-\mu)^2.
$$

Take expectation:

$$
E\left[
\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2
\right]
=
\sum_{i=1}^nE[(X_i-\mu)^2]
-
nE[(\bar X-\mu)^2].
$$

Karena

$$
E[(X_i-\mu)^2]=\sigma^2
$$

dan

$$
E[(\bar X-\mu)^2]
=
\operatorname{Var}(\bar X)
=
\frac{\sigma^2}{n},
$$

maka

$$
E\left[
\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2
\right]
=
n\sigma^2-\sigma^2
=
(n-1)\sigma^2.
$$

Therefore,

$$
E[S^2]
=
\frac1{n-1}(n-1)\sigma^2
=
\boxed{\sigma^2}.
$$

> [!DANGER] Denominator Trap
> - **Unbiased sample variance**:
>   $$
>   S^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2.
>   $$
> - **Normal variance MLE** dari [[4.5 Estimasi Parameter]]:
>   $$
>   \hat\sigma^2_{\text{MLE}}
>   =
>   \frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2.
>   $$
>
> Jangan mencampur objective “maximize likelihood” dengan property “unbiased”.

---

# Section 3 — Asymptotic Unbiasedness

Estimator yang biased untuk finite $n$ dapat menjadi **asymptotically unbiased**.

Miller mendefinisikan:

$$
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}b_n(\theta)=0
}.
$$

Dengan kata lain,

$$
E[\hat\theta_n]-\theta\to0.
$$

### Important Distinction

$$
\text{unbiased}
$$

adalah finite-sample expectation property.

Sedangkan

$$
\text{asymptotically unbiased}
$$

adalah limiting expectation property.

> [!DANGER] Asymptotic-Unbiasedness ≠ Consistency
> Bias menuju nol saja belum otomatis menyatakan estimator mendekati parameter dengan probability menuju 1. Consistency memiliki definition sendiri.

---

# Section 4 — Unbiasedness Tidak Dipertahankan oleh Transformasi

Miller menekankan:

Jika

$$
E[\hat\theta]=\theta,
$$

tidak otomatis berlaku

$$
E[g(\hat\theta)]
=
g(\theta).
$$

Secara umum,

$$
\boxed{
E[g(\hat\theta)]
\ne
g(E[\hat\theta])
}.
$$

Jadi:

$$
\hat\theta\text{ unbiased for }\theta
$$

tidak menjamin

$$
g(\hat\theta)\text{ unbiased for }g(\theta).
$$

Ini penting karena MLE memiliki invariance property, tetapi **unbiasedness does not**.

---

# Section 5 — Minimum Variance & Efficiency

## 5.1 Why Variance Matters

Jika ada dua unbiased estimators untuk parameter yang sama,

$$
\hat\theta_1,
\qquad
\hat\theta_2,
$$

Miller memilih estimator dengan sampling variance lebih kecil sebagai estimator yang lebih “reliable”.

Jika

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_1)
<
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_2),
$$

maka $\hat\theta_1$ lebih terkonsentrasi di sekitar target $\theta$.

---

## 5.2 Minimum Variance Unbiased Estimator

Estimator unbiased yang memiliki variance paling kecil di antara seluruh unbiased estimators disebut **minimum variance unbiased estimator**:

$$
\boxed{
\text{MVUE / best unbiased estimator}
}.
$$

Formal criterion:

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_{\text{MVUE}})
\le
\operatorname{Var}(\tilde\theta)
$$

untuk setiap unbiased estimator $\tilde\theta$ dari parameter yang sama.

> [!IMPORTANT] Comparator Restriction
> “Minimum variance” di sini berarti **minimum di antara unbiased estimators**, bukan minimum di antara semua random variables.

---

# Section 6 — Cramér–Rao Inequality

Miller memberi lower bound untuk variance unbiased estimator.

Jika $\hat\theta$ unbiased dan regularity conditions yang diperlukan berlaku, maka

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)
\ge
\frac{
1
}{
nE_\theta
\left[
\left(
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
\ln f(X;\theta)
\right)^2
\right]
}
}.
$$

Denominator

$$
nE_\theta
\left[
\left(
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
\ln f(X;\theta)
\right)^2
\right]
$$

disebut information about $\theta$ supplied by the sample.

### Equality Case

Jika unbiased estimator mencapai bound:

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)
=
\frac{
1
}{
nE[
(\partial\ln f(X;\theta)/\partial\theta)^2
]
},
$$

maka Miller menyatakan estimator tersebut adalah minimum variance unbiased estimator.

> [!TIP] Exam Workflow — Cramér–Rao
> 1. Write $f(x;\theta)$.
> 2. Compute $\ln f(x;\theta)$.
> 3. Differentiate:
>    $$
>    \frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\ln f(X;\theta).
>    $$
> 4. Square.
> 5. Take expectation.
> 6. Multiply by $n$.
> 7. Invert.
> 8. Compare with actual estimator variance.

---

# Section 7 — Textbook Example: $\bar X$ is MVUE for Normal Mean

Suppose

$$
X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)
$$

with known $\sigma^2$.

PDF kernel:

$$
f(x;\mu)
\propto
\exp\left[
-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}
\right].
$$

Log density:

$$
\ln f(x;\mu)
=
\text{constant}
-
\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}.
$$

Derivative:

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\ln f(x;\mu)
=
\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma^2}.
$$

Square expectation:

$$
E\left[
\left(
\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma^2}
\right)^2
\right]
=
\frac1{\sigma^4}E[(X-\mu)^2]
=
\frac1{\sigma^2}.
$$

Thus Cramér–Rao lower bound:

$$
\frac1{n(1/\sigma^2)}
=
\frac{\sigma^2}{n}.
$$

But

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\bar X)
=
\frac{\sigma^2}{n}.
$$

Since $\bar X$ is unbiased and reaches the bound,

$$
\boxed{
\bar X
\text{ is a minimum variance unbiased estimator of }\mu
}
$$

for this Normal model.

> [!DANGER] Generalization Trap
> Miller explicitly warns not to conclude from this example that $\bar X$ is MVUE for the mean of **every** population.

---

# Section 8 — Relative Efficiency

Suppose $\hat\theta_1$ dan $\hat\theta_2$ adalah unbiased estimators dari parameter yang sama.

Miller uses the ratio

$$
\boxed{
\frac{
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_1)
}{
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_2)
}
}
$$

as the **efficiency of $\hat\theta_2$ relative to $\hat\theta_1$**.

This orientation matters.

### Interpretation

If

$$
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_1)}
{\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_2)}
=
0.25,
$$

then estimator $\hat\theta_2$ has only 25% relative efficiency with respect to $\hat\theta_1$ under Miller's convention, meaning $\hat\theta_2$ has larger variance.

> [!DANGER] Ratio Orientation Trap
> Always identify:
>
> **“efficiency of which estimator relative to which estimator?”**
>
> Do not memorize an unlabeled variance ratio.

---

# Section 9 — Efficiency Example: Uniform Population

For a Uniform population on $(0,\beta)$, Miller compares two unbiased estimators of $\beta$:

$$
\hat\beta_1
=
\frac{n+1}{n}Y_n,
$$

where

$$
Y_n=\max(X_1,\ldots,X_n),
$$

and

$$
\hat\beta_2=2\bar X.
$$

Miller obtains

$$
\operatorname{Var}\left(
\frac{n+1}{n}Y_n
\right)
=
\frac{\beta^2}{n(n+2)}
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{Var}(2\bar X)
=
\frac{\beta^2}{3n}.
$$

Efficiency of $2\bar X$ relative to $\frac{n+1}{n}Y_n$:

$$
\frac{
\beta^2/[n(n+2)]
}{
\beta^2/(3n)
}
=
\boxed{
\frac3{n+2}
}.
$$

For

$$
n=10,
$$

relative efficiency is

$$
\frac3{12}
=
0.25.
$$

Thus order-statistic estimator is much more efficient in this setup.

---

# Section 10 — What If Estimator Is Biased? Use MSE

Miller limits ordinary relative-efficiency comparisons to unbiased estimators.

For a biased estimator, variance alone can be misleading. A constant estimator can have zero variance but be badly wrong.

So use **mean square error (MSE)**:

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{MSE}(\hat\theta)
=
E[
(\hat\theta-\theta)^2
]
}.
$$

Miller gives identity:

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{MSE}(\hat\theta)
=
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)
+
[b(\theta)]^2
}.
$$

Derivation:

$$
\hat\theta-\theta
=
(\hat\theta-E[\hat\theta])
+
(E[\hat\theta]-\theta).
$$

Square and take expectation. Cross term vanishes because

$$
E[\hat\theta-E[\hat\theta]]=0.
$$

Therefore

$$
E[(\hat\theta-\theta)^2]
=
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)
+
(E[\hat\theta]-\theta)^2.
$$

### Special Case

If estimator unbiased,

$$
b(\theta)=0,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{MSE}(\hat\theta)
=
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)
}.
$$

# Section 11 — Consistency

## 11.1 Formal Definition

Miller defines $\hat\theta_n$ as a **consistent estimator** of $\theta$ if, for every

$$
c>0,
$$

$$
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}
P(
|\hat\theta_n-\theta|<c
)
=
1
}.
$$

Equivalent tail form:

$$
\boxed{
P(
|\hat\theta_n-\theta|\ge c
)
\to0
}.
$$

This is exactly

$$
\boxed{
\hat\theta_n\xrightarrow{P}\theta
}.
$$

Consistency adalah **asymptotic property**.

---

## 11.2 Meaning

Consistency says:

> Untuk sample size yang cukup besar, estimator menjadi arbitrarily likely berada dalam tolerance kecil apa pun di sekitar parameter.

Ini berbeda dari unbiasedness.

### Unbiasedness

$$
E[\hat\theta_n]=\theta.
$$

### Consistency

$$
\hat\theta_n\xrightarrow{P}\theta.
$$

Estimator dapat memiliki finite-sample bias namun tetap consistent.

---

# Section 12 — Sufficient Condition for Consistency

Miller gives an immediately useful theorem.

If:

1. $\hat\theta_n$ is unbiased for $\theta$, and
2. 
   $$
   \operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_n)\to0,
   $$

then

$$
\boxed{
\hat\theta_n
\text{ is consistent for }\theta
}.
$$

Reason: Chebyshev inequality:

$$
P(
|\hat\theta_n-\theta|\ge c
)
\le
\frac{
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_n)
}{
c^2
}.
$$

If variance tends to zero, the right side tends to zero.

Miller notes that the theorem also holds with **asymptotically unbiased** replacing unbiased.

> [!IMPORTANT] Sufficient, Not Necessary
> This theorem is a **sufficient condition**, not a definition and not a necessary condition.

---

# Section 13 — Examples of Consistency

## 13.1 Sample Mean

For population with finite variance,

$$
E[\bar X]=\mu
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\bar X)
=
\frac{\sigma^2}{n}
\to0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\bar X\xrightarrow{P}\mu
}.
$$

This links directly to [[4.4 Hukum Bilangan Besar (LLN)]].

---

## 13.2 Sample Proportion

For Bernoulli/Binomial sampling,

$$
\hat p=\frac Xn.
$$

Then

$$
E[\hat p]=p
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\hat p)
=
\frac{p(1-p)}{n}
\to0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\hat p\xrightarrow{P}p
}.
$$

---

## 13.3 Sample Variance under Normality

For Normal population,

$$
E[S^2]=\sigma^2
$$

and from the chi-square result,

$$
\operatorname{Var}(S^2)
=
\frac{2\sigma^4}{n-1}.
$$

As

$$
n\to\infty,
$$

$$
\operatorname{Var}(S^2)\to0.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{
S^2\xrightarrow{P}\sigma^2
}.
$$

---

# Section 14 — Sufficiency

## 14.1 Intuition

Miller describes a sufficient estimator/statistic as one that uses **all information in the sample relevant to estimating $\theta$**.

If statistic

$$
T=T(X_1,\ldots,X_n)
$$

is sufficient, then once $T$ is known, looking again at the full sample values gives no additional information about $\theta$.

Mental model:

```text
full sample
(X1,...,Xn)
       ↓ compress
       T
       ↓
all parameter-relevant information retained
```

> [!IMPORTANT] Sufficiency ≠ Accuracy
> A statistic can be sufficient without being unbiased or minimum-variance.
>
> Sufficiency is an **information-retention property**, not directly an error-size property.

---

# Section 15 — Formal Conditional Definition of Sufficiency

Miller's formal definition:

A statistic $\hat\theta=T(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ is sufficient for parameter $\theta$ if, for every value $t$ of the statistic, the conditional distribution of the complete sample given

$$
T=t
$$

is independent of $\theta$.

Symbolically:

$$
\boxed{
f(
x_1,\ldots,x_n
\mid
T=t;
\theta
)
\text{ does not depend on }\theta
}.
$$

Interpretation:

Once $T$ is fixed, the remaining way in which the sample can be arranged carries no further parameter information.

### To Show Not Sufficient

Miller notes that the direct definition is often useful if you want to show **non-sufficiency**:

Find a conditional sample probability/density given $T=t$ that still depends on $\theta$.

---

# Section 16 — Bernoulli Example of Sufficiency

Let

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n
\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}
\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\theta).
$$

The joint PMF is

$$
f(x_1,\ldots,x_n;\theta)
=
\prod_{i=1}^n
\theta^{x_i}
(1-\theta)^{1-x_i}.
$$

Collect powers:

$$
=
\theta^{\sum x_i}
(1-\theta)^{n-\sum x_i}.
$$

Thus data depend on $\theta$ only through

$$
\sum_{i=1}^nX_i
$$

or equivalently

$$
\bar X.
$$

Miller formally shows via the conditional distribution that sample proportion

$$
\boxed{
\bar X
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nX_i
}
$$

is sufficient for $\theta$.

Given a fixed total number of successes, the conditional probability of any compatible ordered Bernoulli sequence becomes independent of $\theta$.

---

# Section 17 — Factorization Theorem

Checking the conditional definition directly can be tedious.

Miller gives the factorization theorem:

A statistic

$$
T=T(X_1,\ldots,X_n)
$$

is sufficient for $\theta$ iff the joint PMF/PDF can be written as

$$
\boxed{
f(x_1,\ldots,x_n;\theta)
=
g(T(x_1,\ldots,x_n),\theta)
\,
h(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
}
$$

where:

- $g$ depends on the sample only through $T$ and may depend on $\theta$;
- $h$ does **not** depend on $\theta$.

### Exam Recognition Rule

```text
joint likelihood
      ↓
factor all theta-dependent terms
      ↓
do those terms depend on data only through T?
      ↓ yes
T sufficient
```

> [!TIP] Fast Sufficiency Test
> For iid samples:
> 1. Write
>    $$
>    \prod_i f(x_i;\theta).
>    $$
> 2. Collect parameter-dependent data expressions.
> 3. Identify statistic summarizing those expressions.

---

# Section 18 — Bernoulli Sufficiency via Factorization

For Bernoulli sample,

$$
f(\mathbf{x};\theta)
=
\theta^{\sum x_i}
(1-\theta)^{n-\sum x_i}.
$$

Choose

$$
T(\mathbf{x})
=
\sum_{i=1}^n x_i.
$$

Then

$$
g(T,\theta)
=
\theta^T(1-\theta)^{n-T},
$$

and

$$
h(\mathbf{x})=1
$$

on valid Bernoulli support.

Thus

$$
\boxed{
\sum X_i
\text{ is sufficient for }\theta
}.
$$

Since

$$
\bar X=\frac1n\sum X_i
$$

is a one-to-one transformation of $\sum X_i$ for fixed $n$, Miller's discussion implies $\bar X$ also preserves sufficiency.

---

# Section 19 — Normal Mean with Known Variance

Suppose

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n
\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}
N(\mu,\sigma^2)
$$

with known $\sigma^2$.

Joint density:

$$
f(\mathbf{x};\mu)
=
\left(
\frac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
\right)^n
\exp\left[
-\frac1{2\sigma^2}
\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-\mu)^2
\right].
$$

Use identity:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-\mu)^2
=
\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-\bar x)^2
+
n(\bar x-\mu)^2.
$$

Therefore

$$
f(\mathbf{x};\mu)
=
\underbrace{
\exp\left[
-\frac{n(\bar x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}
\right]
}_{g(\bar x,\mu)}
\cdot
\underbrace{
\left(
\frac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
\right)^n
\exp\left[
-\frac1{2\sigma^2}
\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2
\right]
}_{h(\mathbf{x})}.
$$

Second factor does not involve $\mu$.

Hence

$$
\boxed{
\bar X
\text{ is sufficient for }\mu
}
$$

when $\sigma^2$ is known.

# Section 20 — Sufficiency and Hypothesis Testing

Hogg, McKean & Craig §8.2 makes an important observation.

If $T$ is sufficient, factorization gives

$$
L(\theta;\mathbf{x})
=
k_1(T(\mathbf{x});\theta)
k_2(\mathbf{x}),
$$

with $k_2$ independent of $\theta$.

For two parameter values $\theta'$ and $\theta''$,

$$
\frac{
L(\theta';\mathbf{x})
}{
L(\theta'';\mathbf{x})
}
=
\frac{
k_1(T;\theta')
}{
k_1(T;\theta'')
}.
$$

The $k_2$ terms cancel.

Thus likelihood-ratio decisions can be based on the sufficient statistic alone.

Meaning:

> Sufficiency is not just a compression concept. It can reduce inference/testing problems to a lower-dimensional statistic **without losing parameter-relevant information**.

# Section 24 — Worked Exam-Style Cases

## Case A — Check Unbiasedness

Let

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n
$$

be iid with

$$
E[X_i]=\mu.
$$

Consider

$$
T=2\bar X.
$$

Then

$$
E[T]
=
2E[\bar X]
=
2\mu.
$$

So as estimator of $\mu$,

$$
b(\mu)
=
2\mu-\mu
=
\mu.
$$

Thus $T$ is biased unless $\mu=0$.

> [!WARNING]
> Being correct at $\mu=0$ does not make $T$ unbiased over the parameter space.

---

## Case B — Compare Two Unbiased Estimators

Suppose

$$
E[T_1]=E[T_2]=\theta
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{Var}(T_1)=\frac4n,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(T_2)=\frac6n.
$$

Since

$$
\frac4n<\frac6n,
$$

$T_1$ is more efficient in the ordinary variance sense.

Efficiency of $T_2$ relative to $T_1$ under Miller convention:

$$
\frac{
\operatorname{Var}(T_1)
}{
\operatorname{Var}(T_2)
}
=
\frac{4/n}{6/n}
=
\boxed{\frac23}.
$$

---

## Case C — Biased Estimator and MSE

Suppose

$$
E[T]=\theta+\frac1n
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{Var}(T)=\frac4n.
$$

Bias:

$$
b_n(\theta)
=
\frac1n.
$$

MSE:

$$
\operatorname{MSE}(T)
=
\frac4n
+
\frac1{n^2}.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{MSE}(T)
=
\frac4n+\frac1{n^2}
}.
$$

The estimator is biased for finite $n$ but asymptotically unbiased because

$$
\frac1n\to0.
$$

---

## Case D — Prove Consistency by Chebyshev Route

Suppose

$$
E[T_n]=\theta
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{Var}(T_n)
=
\frac{9}{n}.
$$

For any $c>0$,

$$
P(|T_n-\theta|\ge c)
\le
\frac{9}{nc^2}.
$$

As

$$
n\to\infty,
$$

right-hand side tends to zero.

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
T_n\xrightarrow{P}\theta
}.
$$

---

## Case E — Sufficiency for Poisson Mean

Suppose

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n
\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}
\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda).
$$

Joint PMF:

$$
L(\lambda;\mathbf{x})
=
\prod_{i=1}^n
\frac{
e^{-\lambda}\lambda^{x_i}
}{
x_i!
}.
$$

Collect terms:

$$
=
e^{-n\lambda}
\lambda^{\sum x_i}
\cdot
\frac1{
\prod x_i!
}.
$$

Let

$$
T=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i.
$$

Then

$$
g(T,\lambda)
=
e^{-n\lambda}\lambda^T,
$$

and

$$
h(\mathbf{x})
=
\frac1{\prod x_i!}
$$

does not depend on $\lambda$.

By factorization theorem,

$$
\boxed{
\sum X_i
\text{ is sufficient for }\lambda
}.
$$

# Section 25 — Exam Traps & Error Prevention

## 25.1 Unbiased = Exact for Every Sample?

Wrong.

Unbiased means:

$$
E[\hat\theta]=\theta,
$$

not

$$
\hat\theta=\theta
$$

for every sample.

---

## 25.2 Small Variance but Huge Bias

Variance alone cannot evaluate a biased estimator.

Use

$$
\operatorname{MSE}
=
\operatorname{Var}
+
\text{Bias}^2.
$$

---

## 25.3 Efficiency Without Stating Comparator

Always label relative efficiency.

Variance ratio without wording is ambiguous.

---

## 25.4 Cramér–Rao Bound Applied Automatically

The source states the bound under general regularity conditions. Do not use it mechanically when support/endpoints depend on parameter unless the required conditions are satisfied.

---

## 25.5 Consistency from Unbiasedness Alone

Wrong.

Unbiasedness alone does not make estimator consistent.

A source-supported sufficient route is:

$$
E[\hat\theta_n]=\theta
$$

plus

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta_n)\to0.
$$

---

## 25.6 Consistency = Bias Goes to Zero?

Wrong.

Asymptotic unbiasedness concerns expectation. Consistency concerns probability concentration.

---

## 25.7 Sufficiency = “Good Estimator”

Wrong.

Sufficiency says full sample contains no additional information about $\theta$ once statistic is known.

It does not directly say small bias or variance.

---

## 25.8 Factorization Trap

For

$$
f(\mathbf{x};\theta)
=
g(T,\theta)h(\mathbf{x}),
$$

the function

$$
h(\mathbf{x})
$$

must not depend on $\theta$.

---

## 25.9 Statistic Must Not Depend on Unknown Parameter

A candidate statistic used for sufficiency must be a function of sample observations; do not hide unknown $\theta$ inside its definition.

---

## 25.10 Completeness Overreach

Silabus requires recognition of completeness, but the available official Topik 4 chapters do not provide enough source material for a formal calculation framework.

Do not silently invent one.

---
