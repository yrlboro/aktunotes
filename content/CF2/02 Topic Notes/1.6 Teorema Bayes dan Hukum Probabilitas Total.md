# Section 1 — Intuisi & Big Picture

Bayes problems hampir selalu memiliki dua arah probability:

1. **forward probability**  
   probability evidence terjadi jika source/cause tertentu benar:
   $$
   P(A\mid B_i);
   $$

2. **reverse probability**  
   probability source/cause tertentu setelah evidence diamati:
   $$
   P(B_i\mid A).
   $$

Teorema Bayes mengubah arah pertama menjadi arah kedua.

Tetapi untuk melakukan reversal, kita harus memperhitungkan:

- **prior/base rate** $P(B_i)$;
- seberapa mungkin evidence muncul dari setiap source, $P(A\mid B_i)$;
- seluruh cara evidence $A$ dapat terjadi.

Mental model utama:

```text
Possible source/cause B1, B2, ..., Bm
        ↓
prior probabilities P(Bi)
        ↓
likelihood-like conditional P(A | Bi)
        ↓
joint contribution P(Bi)P(A | Bi)
        ↓
sum all contributions → P(A)
        ↓
normalize desired contribution
        ↓
posterior P(Bk | A)
```

> [!TIP] Core CF2 Rule
> **Bayes = desired path / all paths producing the observed evidence.**

# Section 2 — Partition: Struktur yang Membuat Total Probability Bekerja

Events

$$
B_1,B_2,\ldots,B_m
$$

membentuk **partition** dari sample space $S$ jika:

### 1. Mutually exclusive

$$
B_i\cap B_j=\varnothing,
\qquad i\ne j.
$$

### 2. Exhaustive

$$
B_1\cup B_2\cup\cdots\cup B_m=S.
$$

Artinya setiap outcome berasal dari **tepat satu** $B_i$.

Jika event $A$ dapat terjadi, maka:

$$
A
=
(A\cap B_1)
\cup
(A\cap B_2)
\cup\cdots\cup
(A\cap B_m).
$$

Karena pieces tersebut mutually exclusive:

$$
P(A)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{m}P(A\cap B_i).
$$

Dengan multiplication rule:

$$
P(A\cap B_i)
=
P(B_i)P(A\mid B_i).
$$

Sehingga lahirlah hukum probabilitas total.

---

# Section 3 — Hukum Probabilitas Total

Jika $B_1,\ldots,B_m$ merupakan partition dari $S$ dan $P(B_i)>0$, maka untuk event $A$:

$$
\boxed{
P(A)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{m}
P(B_i)P(A\mid B_i)
}
$$

atau secara expanded:

$$
\boxed{
P(A)
=
P(B_1)P(A\mid B_1)
+\cdots+
P(B_m)P(A\mid B_m)
}.
$$

Miller juga menyebutnya **rule of total probability** atau **rule of elimination**.

---

## 3.1 Two-Branch Special Case

Jika partition hanya:

$$
B,\quad B^c,
$$

maka:

$$
\boxed{
P(A)
=
P(B)P(A\mid B)
+
P(B^c)P(A\mid B^c)
}
$$

dengan

$$
P(B^c)=1-P(B).
$$

Ini adalah bentuk yang sangat umum dalam:

- defective vs non-defective source;
- disease vs no disease;
- strike vs no strike;
- insured risk class A vs non-A;
- fraud vs legitimate claim.

---

# Section 4 — Mengapa Total Probability Benar?

Start with partition:

$$
S=B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_m.
$$

Then:

$$
A=A\cap S.
$$

Substitute:

$$
A
=
A\cap(B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_m).
$$

By distributive law:

$$
A
=
(A\cap B_1)\cup\cdots\cup(A\cap B_m).
$$

Since $B_i$ are mutually exclusive, so are $A\cap B_i$.

Therefore:

$$
P(A)
=
\sum_{i=1}^m P(A\cap B_i).
$$

Using:

$$
P(A\cap B_i)=P(B_i)P(A\mid B_i),
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
P(A)
=
\sum_{i=1}^mP(B_i)P(A\mid B_i).
}
$$

> [!IMPORTANT] Exam Interpretation
> Total probability adalah **weighted average** dari conditional probabilities $P(A\mid B_i)$, dengan weights $P(B_i)$ yang menjumlah ke 1.

Karena itu:

$$
\min_i P(A\mid B_i)
\le
P(A)
\le
\max_i P(A\mid B_i).
$$

Ini sangat berguna untuk sanity check.

---

# Section 5 — Teorema Bayes

Suppose $B_1,\ldots,B_m$ forms a partition dan $P(A)>0$.

Dari conditional probability:

$$
P(B_k\mid A)
=
\frac{P(B_k\cap A)}{P(A)}.
$$

Numerator:

$$
P(B_k\cap A)
=
P(B_k)P(A\mid B_k).
$$

Denominator dari total probability:

$$
P(A)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{m}
P(B_i)P(A\mid B_i).
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P(B_k\mid A)
=
\frac{
P(B_k)P(A\mid B_k)
}{
\displaystyle
\sum_{i=1}^{m}
P(B_i)P(A\mid B_i)
}
}
$$

This is **Bayes' theorem**.

---

# Section 6 — Prior, Joint Contribution, dan Posterior

Hogg menggunakan terminology:

### Prior probability

$$
\boxed{P(B_i)}
$$

Probability source/hypothesis sebelum evidence $A$ diamati.

### Conditional evidence probability

$$
\boxed{P(A\mid B_i)}
$$

Probability evidence $A$ jika source $B_i$ adalah source yang benar.

### Joint contribution

$$
\boxed{
P(B_i\cap A)
=
P(B_i)P(A\mid B_i)
}
$$

Mass probability dari path:

```text
Bi → A
```

### Evidence / normalizing probability

$$
\boxed{
P(A)
=
\sum_iP(B_i)P(A\mid B_i)
}
$$

### Posterior probability

$$
\boxed{
P(B_i\mid A)
}
$$

Updated probability source $B_i$ setelah evidence $A$ diamati.

---

## 6.1 Bayes as Reweighting

Define unnormalized weight:

$$
w_i
=
P(B_i)P(A\mid B_i).
$$

Then:

$$
P(A)=\sum_iw_i
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
P(B_i\mid A)
=
\frac{w_i}{\sum_jw_j}
}.
$$

This is the cleanest computational interpretation.

> [!TIP] Fast Bayes
> 1. Compute each path weight.  
> 2. Sum all weights.  
> 3. Divide target weight by total.

---

# Section 7 — Probability Tree Mental Model

Untuk three-source case:

```text
                 ┌─ A       P(B1)P(A|B1)
          B1 ────┤
                 └─ Aᶜ

                 ┌─ A       P(B2)P(A|B2)
Start ─── B2 ────┤
                 └─ Aᶜ

                 ┌─ A       P(B3)P(A|B3)
          B3 ────┤
                 └─ Aᶜ
```

Probability $A$ adalah jumlah seluruh terminal paths menuju $A$:

$$
P(A)
=
P(B_1)P(A\mid B_1)
+
P(B_2)P(A\mid B_2)
+
P(B_3)P(A\mid B_3).
$$

Posterior source $B_2$ given $A$:

$$
P(B_2\mid A)
=
\frac{\text{path }B_2\to A}
{\text{all paths ending in }A}.
$$

> [!BUG] Denominator Trap
> Denominator Bayes **bukan** sekadar prior target dan bukan $P(A\mid B_k)$.  
> Denominator harus mencakup **seluruh mutually exclusive paths yang dapat menghasilkan evidence $A$**.

# Section 8 — Textbook Example: Three Bowls

Hogg considers bowls $B_1,B_2,B_3$ selected with:

$$
P(B_1)=\frac13,
$$

$$
P(B_2)=\frac16,
$$

$$
P(B_3)=\frac12.
$$

Conditional probabilities of drawing a red chip $R$:

$$
P(R\mid B_1)=\frac26=\frac13,
$$

$$
P(R\mid B_2)=\frac13,
$$

$$
P(R\mid B_3)=\frac59.
$$

## Step 1 — Total Probability of Red

$$
P(R)
=
P(B_1)P(R\mid B_1)
+
P(B_2)P(R\mid B_2)
+
P(B_3)P(R\mid B_3).
$$

Thus:

$$
P(R)
=
\left(\frac13\right)\left(\frac13\right)
+
\left(\frac16\right)\left(\frac13\right)
+
\left(\frac12\right)\left(\frac59\right).
$$

$$
=
\frac19+\frac1{18}+\frac5{18}
=
\frac49.
$$

## Step 2 — Posterior for $B_1$

$$
P(B_1\mid R)
=
\frac{
P(B_1)P(R\mid B_1)
}{
P(R)
}.
$$

$$
=
\frac{(1/3)(1/3)}{4/9}
=
\frac14.
$$

Hogg's posterior results are:

$$
\boxed{
P(B_1\mid R)=\frac28=\frac14
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
P(B_2\mid R)=\frac18
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
P(B_3\mid R)=\frac58.
}
$$

Check:

$$
\frac28+\frac18+\frac58=1.
$$

### Interpretation

Before seeing red:

$$
P(B_3)=\frac12.
$$

After observing red:

$$
P(B_3\mid R)=\frac58.
$$

Posterior rises because bowl $B_3$ has a relatively high red-chip proportion.

# Section 9 — Textbook Example: Factory Machines

Hogg gives three machines:

| Machine | Share of Total Production | Defective Rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| I | $0.35$ | $0.02$ |
| II | $0.25$ | $0.01$ |
| III | $0.40$ | $0.03$ |

Let $D$ = selected spring is defective.

## 9.1 Total Probability

$$
P(D)
=
(0.35)(0.02)
+
(0.25)(0.01)
+
(0.40)(0.03).
$$

Compute:

$$
P(D)
=
0.007+0.0025+0.012
=
0.0215.
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
P(D)=\frac{215}{10{,}000}=0.0215.
}
$$

---

## 9.2 Posterior Machine III Given Defective

$$
P(III\mid D)
=
\frac{
P(III)P(D\mid III)
}{
P(D)
}.
$$

$$
=
\frac{(0.40)(0.03)}{0.0215}
=
\frac{0.012}{0.0215}
=
\frac{120}{215}.
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
P(III\mid D)\approx0.5581.
}
$$

Prior:

$$
P(III)=0.40.
$$

Posterior:

$$
P(III\mid D)\approx0.558.
$$

The defective observation makes machine III more plausible because its defective rate is largest.

# Section 10 — Textbook Example: Miller Rental Agencies

Miller gives:

| Agency | Rental Share | Probability Car Needs Oil Change |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | $0.60$ | $0.09$ |
| 2 | $0.30$ | $0.20$ |
| 3 | $0.10$ | $0.06$ |

Let $A$ = rental car needs an oil change.

Total probability:

$$
P(A)
=
(0.60)(0.09)
+
(0.30)(0.20)
+
(0.10)(0.06).
$$

$$
=
0.054+0.060+0.006
=
0.12.
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{P(A)=0.12.}
$$

If the car needs an oil change, posterior probability it came from agency 2 is:

$$
P(B_2\mid A)
=
\frac{(0.30)(0.20)}{0.12}.
$$

$$
=
\frac{0.06}{0.12}
=
\boxed{0.50}.
$$

Notice the update:

$$
P(B_2)=0.30
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
P(B_2\mid A)=0.50.
$$

Agency 2 becomes more likely after observing the oil-change evidence because its conditional oil-change rate is relatively high.

# Section 11 — Base Rate Effect

Bayes forces us to combine:

$$
\text{base rate}
\times
\text{conditional evidence rate}.
$$

A very high value of:

$$
P(A\mid B)
$$

does **not** necessarily imply a high posterior:

$$
P(B\mid A).
$$

If prior:

$$
P(B)
$$

is extremely small, another much more common category may generate more $A$ outcomes overall.

Hogg's screening example illustrates exactly this phenomenon.

> [!DANGER] Base-Rate Trap
> Do not confuse:
>
> $$
> P(\text{positive}\mid\text{condition})
> $$
>
> with:
>
> $$
> P(\text{condition}\mid\text{positive}).
> $$
>
> Sensitivity-like information alone is insufficient; the prior/base rate and false-positive path also matter.

# Section 12 — Case A — Fundamental

### Soal

A construction job may be delayed by a strike.

Given:

$$
P(S)=0.60,
$$

where $S$ = strike.

Probability job finishes on time $A$:

$$
P(A\mid S)=0.35,
$$

$$
P(A\mid S^c)=0.85.
$$

Find $P(A)$.

> [!SUCCESS] Pembahasan
>
> **1. Parse Narasi & Target**
>
> Target:
>
> $$
> P(A).
> $$
>
> **2. Identify Structure**
>
> The intermediate event has two exhaustive states:
>
> $$
> S,\quad S^c.
> $$
>
> They form a partition.
>
> **3. Support / Event Structure**
>
> Event $A$ can occur through:
>
> $$
> A\cap S
> $$
>
> or
>
> $$
> A\cap S^c.
> $$
>
> These paths are mutually exclusive.
>
> **4. Setup**
>
> $$
> P(A)
> =
> P(S)P(A\mid S)
> +
> P(S^c)P(A\mid S^c).
> $$
>
> **5. Calculation**
>
> $$
> P(S^c)=1-0.60=0.40.
> $$
>
> Therefore:
>
> $$
> P(A)
> =
> (0.60)(0.35)
> +
> (0.40)(0.85).
> $$
>
> $$
> =0.21+0.34
> =\boxed{0.55}.
> $$
>
> **6. Interpretation**
>
> Overall probability of completing the job on time is $55\%$.
>
> **7. Verification**
>
> Because:
>
> $$
> 0.35\le0.55\le0.85,
> $$
>
> the total probability lies between the two conditional probabilities, consistent with a weighted average.

> [!WARNING] Exam Trap — Case A
> - **Trap:** Use $0.60(0.35)+0.60(0.85)$.
> - **Why tempting:** $0.60$ is the only explicit prior shown first.
> - **Why wrong:** second branch is $S^c$, whose probability is $0.40$.
> - **Shortcut aman:** draw two branches $S$ and $S^c$.
> - **Target waktu:** ~45–60 seconds.

# Section 13 — Case B — Exam-Typical

### Soal

A company purchases bean seeds from two suppliers:

$$
P(A)=0.40,
\qquad
P(B)=0.60.
$$

Germination rates:

$$
P(G\mid A)=0.85,
$$

$$
P(G\mid B)=0.75.
$$

Find:

1. $P(G)$;
2. $P(A\mid G)$.

> [!SUCCESS] Pembahasan
>
> **1. Parse Narasi & Target**
>
> Need first the overall evidence probability:
>
> $$
> P(G).
> $$
>
> Then reverse conditioning:
>
> $$
> P(A\mid G).
> $$
>
> **2. Identify Structure**
>
> Supplier events:
>
> $$
> A,\quad B
> $$
>
> form a partition.
>
> **3. Support / Event Paths**
>
> A germinating seed can come from exactly one of:
>
> $$
> A\cap G
> $$
>
> or:
>
> $$
> B\cap G.
> $$
>
> **4. Setup**
>
> Total probability:
>
> $$
> P(G)
> =
> P(A)P(G\mid A)
> +
> P(B)P(G\mid B).
> $$
>
> Bayes:
>
> $$
> P(A\mid G)
> =
> \frac{P(A)P(G\mid A)}{P(G)}.
> $$
>
> **5. Calculation**
>
> $$
> P(G)
> =
> (0.40)(0.85)
> +
> (0.60)(0.75).
> $$
>
> $$
> =0.34+0.45
> =\boxed{0.79}.
> $$
>
> Then:
>
> $$
> P(A\mid G)
> =
> \frac{0.34}{0.79}
> =
> \boxed{\frac{34}{79}\approx0.4304}.
> $$
>
> **6. Interpretation**
>
> Supplier A originally supplies $40\%$ of seeds. After observing successful germination, its posterior rises to about $43.0\%$, because its germination rate is higher than supplier B's.
>
> **7. Verification**
>
> $P(G)=0.79$ lies between $0.75$ and $0.85$.
>
> Posterior:
>
> $$
> 0<P(A\mid G)<1.
> $$

> [!WARNING] Exam Trap — Case B
> - **Trap:** report $P(G\mid A)=0.85$ as $P(A\mid G)$.
> - **Why tempting:** both statements mention supplier A and germination.
> - **Why wrong:** conditioning direction is reversed.
> - **Shortcut aman:** Bayes numerator is always the **joint path matching target + evidence**.
> - **Target waktu:** ~60–90 seconds.

# Section 14 — Case C — Challenging / Integrated

### Soal

Three machines produce a factory's springs:

$$
P(I)=0.35,
\quad
P(II)=0.25,
\quad
P(III)=0.40.
$$

Defective rates:

$$
P(D\mid I)=0.02,
$$

$$
P(D\mid II)=0.01,
$$

$$
P(D\mid III)=0.03.
$$

A spring is selected and found defective.

Find:

1. $P(D)$;
2. $P(I\mid D)$;
3. $P(II\mid D)$;
4. $P(III\mid D)$;
5. verify posterior probabilities sum to 1.

> [!SUCCESS] Pembahasan
>
> **1. Parse Narasi & Target**
>
> The observed evidence is:
>
> $$
> D.
> $$
>
> Need posterior distribution across the three machine sources.
>
> **2. Identify Structure**
>
> $I,II,III$ are mutually exclusive and exhaustive production sources.
>
> **3. Support / Event Paths**
>
> All defective paths are:
>
> $$
> I\to D,\quad II\to D,\quad III\to D.
> $$
>
> **4. Setup**
>
> Path weights:
>
> $$
> w_I=P(I)P(D\mid I),
> $$
>
> $$
> w_{II}=P(II)P(D\mid II),
> $$
>
> $$
> w_{III}=P(III)P(D\mid III).
> $$
>
> Then:
>
> $$
> P(D)=w_I+w_{II}+w_{III}.
> $$
>
> Posterior:
>
> $$
> P(j\mid D)=\frac{w_j}{P(D)}.
> $$
>
> **5. Calculation**
>
> Joint weights:
>
> $$
> w_I=(0.35)(0.02)=0.007,
> $$
>
> $$
> w_{II}=(0.25)(0.01)=0.0025,
> $$
>
> $$
> w_{III}=(0.40)(0.03)=0.012.
> $$
>
> Therefore:
>
> $$
> P(D)
> =
> 0.007+0.0025+0.012
> =
> \boxed{0.0215}.
> $$
>
> Machine I:
>
> $$
> P(I\mid D)
> =
> \frac{0.007}{0.0215}
> =
> \frac{70}{215}
> \approx
> \boxed{0.3256}.
> $$
>
> Machine II:
>
> $$
> P(II\mid D)
> =
> \frac{0.0025}{0.0215}
> =
> \frac{25}{215}
> \approx
> \boxed{0.1163}.
> $$
>
> Machine III:
>
> $$
> P(III\mid D)
> =
> \frac{0.012}{0.0215}
> =
> \frac{120}{215}
> \approx
> \boxed{0.5581}.
> $$
>
> **6. Interpretation**
>
> Before seeing the defective spring, machine III has prior probability $0.40$. After observing defectiveness, its probability rises to about $0.5581$, because it combines both substantial production volume and the highest defective rate.
>
> **7. Verification**
>
> $$
> \frac{70+25+120}{215}
> =
> \frac{215}{215}
> =
> 1.
> $$
>
> So posterior probabilities are properly normalized.

> [!WARNING] Exam Trap — Case C
> - **Trap:** choose machine III simply because $3\%$ is the largest defective rate, without accounting for production shares.
> - **Why tempting:** conditional probability $P(D\mid III)$ looks like the direct answer.
> - **Why wrong:** posterior depends on **prior × conditional rate**.
> - **Shortcut aman:** make a path-weight column and normalize.
> - **Target waktu:** ~90–120 seconds.

# Section 15 — Verification & Sanity Check

> [!CHECK] Sanity Check

### Check 1 — Partition probabilities sum to 1

Before total probability:

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^{m}P(B_i)=1.
}
$$

If not, either:

- partition is incomplete;
- categories overlap;
- data transcription is wrong.

---

### Check 2 — Total probability must lie in $[0,1]$

$$
0\le P(A)\le1.
$$

Additionally, because it is a weighted average:

$$
\min_iP(A\mid B_i)
\le P(A)\le
\max_iP(A\mid B_i).
$$

---

### Check 3 — Posterior probabilities must sum to 1

If $B_1,\ldots,B_m$ forms a complete partition:

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^{m}P(B_i\mid A)=1.
}
$$

This is one of the strongest Bayes checks.

---

### Check 4 — Numerator cannot exceed denominator

For each $k$:

$$
P(B_k)P(A\mid B_k)
=
P(B_k\cap A)
\le
P(A).
$$

Therefore:

$$
P(B_k\mid A)\le1.
$$

---

### Check 5 — Direction of update should make qualitative sense

If category $B_k$ produces evidence $A$ at a rate **above the overall rate**:

$$
P(A\mid B_k)>P(A),
$$

then observing $A$ tends to increase its probability:

$$
P(B_k\mid A)>P(B_k).
$$

Indeed:

$$
\frac{P(B_k\mid A)}{P(B_k)}
=
\frac{P(A\mid B_k)}{P(A)}.
$$

This ratio is a useful qualitative diagnostic.

---

## Metode Alternatif — Tree vs Formula

### Formula Method

Fastest when probabilities are already tabulated:

$$
P(B_k\mid A)
=
\frac{
P(B_k)P(A\mid B_k)
}{
\sum_iP(B_i)P(A\mid B_i)
}.
$$

### Tree Method

Better when:

- many sequential stages;
- user could confuse conditioning direction;
- complements appear;
- different branches have different probabilities.

Both methods are mathematically identical.

> [!TIP] Exam Choice
> For 2–4 simple sources, a quick **path-weight table** is usually cleaner than drawing a full tree.

---

# Section 16 — Path-Weight Table Method

For each source:

| Source | Prior | Evidence Probability | Joint Weight |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $B_1$ | $P(B_1)$ | $P(A\mid B_1)$ | $P(B_1)P(A\mid B_1)$ |
| $\vdots$ | $\vdots$ | $\vdots$ | $\vdots$ |
| $B_m$ | $P(B_m)$ | $P(A\mid B_m)$ | $P(B_m)P(A\mid B_m)$ |
| **Total** | $1$ | — | $P(A)$ |

Then:

$$
P(B_k\mid A)
=
\frac{\text{target row weight}}
{\text{total weight}}.
$$

This format minimizes denominator errors.

# Section 17 — Exam Traps

## 17.1 Reverse-Conditional Trap

Given:

$$
P(A\mid B),
$$

question asks:

$$
P(B\mid A).
$$

These are not equal in general.

Bayes is required.

---

## 17.2 Base-Rate Neglect

Wrong mental shortcut:

> “$A$ is very likely under $B$, therefore $B$ is very likely after observing $A$.”

Posterior requires:

$$
P(B)P(A\mid B).
$$

A rare $B$ can still have a small posterior.

---

## 17.3 Missing Branch Trap

If $B_1,\ldots,B_m$ are the ways evidence can occur, denominator must include **all** of them:

$$
\sum_iP(B_i)P(A\mid B_i).
$$

Omitting one path biases every posterior.

---

## 17.4 Non-Partition Trap

Total probability formula assumes source events are:

- mutually exclusive;
- exhaustive.

If categories overlap, direct use of the formula double counts.

---

## 17.5 Complement Trap

For two categories:

$$
P(B^c)=1-P(B).
$$

If the question gives only $P(B)$, remember to construct the complement branch explicitly.

---

## 17.6 Joint-vs-Conditional Trap

Bayes numerator is:

$$
P(B_k\cap A)
=
P(B_k)P(A\mid B_k),
$$

not just:

$$
P(A\mid B_k).
$$

---

## 17.7 Normalize Too Early Trap

Safe workflow:

1. calculate all unnormalized weights;
2. sum them;
3. normalize only once.

This reduces repetitive fraction mistakes.

---

# Section 18 — Formula Map

| Concept | Formula | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Partition | $\cup_iB_i=S$, $B_i\cap B_j=\varnothing$ | mutually exclusive & exhaustive |
| Total probability | $P(A)=\sum_iP(B_i)P(A\mid B_i)$ | $B_i$ partition |
| Two-branch total probability | $P(A)=P(B)P(A\mid B)+P(B^c)P(A\mid B^c)$ | $B,B^c$ partition |
| Joint path | $P(B_i\cap A)=P(B_i)P(A\mid B_i)$ | multiplication rule |
| Bayes theorem | $P(B_k\mid A)=\dfrac{P(B_k)P(A\mid B_k)}{\sum_iP(B_i)P(A\mid B_i)}$ | $P(A)>0$ |
| Prior | $P(B_i)$ | before evidence |
| Posterior | $P(B_i\mid A)$ | after evidence |
| Evidence probability | $P(A)=\sum_iw_i$ | normalizing constant |
| Path weight | $w_i=P(B_i)P(A\mid B_i)$ | unnormalized posterior weight |

# Section 19 — Connections & Mental Model

## 19.1 Connection to [[1.4 Probabilitas Bersyarat]]

Bayes starts from:

$$
P(B_k\mid A)
=
\frac{P(B_k\cap A)}{P(A)}.
$$

Thus Bayes is not a separate probability system; it is a consequence of conditional probability plus total probability.

---

## 19.2 Connection to [[1.5 Kejadian Independen]]

If $A$ and $B$ are independent:

$$
P(B\mid A)=P(B).
$$

In such a case, evidence $A$ causes no probability update about $B$.

Bayes becomes most informative when evidence changes relative plausibility across source categories.

---

## 19.3 Connection to [[4.5 Estimasi Parameter]]

Later Bayesian estimation uses the same conceptual update:

```text
prior
×
data information
→
posterior
```

But Topik 4.5 treats unknown statistical parameters and formal prior distributions; do not import those concepts back into Topik 1.6 unless explicitly needed.
