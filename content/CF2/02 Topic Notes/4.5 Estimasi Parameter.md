## Section 1 — Intuisi & Big Picture

Dalam statistical inference, population distribution memiliki satu atau lebih **unknown parameters**. Misalkan model ditulis

$$
f(x;\theta),
$$

dengan $\theta$ parameter yang tidak diketahui.

Kita tidak mengamati $\theta$ secara langsung. Yang tersedia adalah random sample

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n.
$$

Sebuah **estimator** adalah statistic yang menggunakan sample tersebut untuk menebak parameter:

$$
\hat\theta=T(X_1,\ldots,X_n).
$$

Sebelum data diamati, $\hat\theta$ adalah random variable. Setelah realization

$$
X_i=x_i
$$

diamati, diperoleh **estimate**

$$
\hat\theta_{\text{obs}}=T(x_1,\ldots,x_n).
$$

> [!IMPORTANT] Parameter vs Estimator vs Estimate
> $$
> \theta
> \quad\neq\quad
> \hat\theta
> \quad\neq\quad
> \hat\theta_{\text{obs}}.
> $$
>
> - $\theta$: fixed unknown population parameter dalam frequentist setup.
> - $\hat\theta$: random statistic/estimator.
> - $\hat\theta_{\text{obs}}$: realized numerical estimate.

Miller mendefinisikan **point estimation** sebagai penggunaan nilai sebuah sample statistic untuk mengestimasi population parameter. Disebut *point* karena hasilnya satu angka, berbeda dari interval estimation yang akan muncul di [[4.7 Selang Kepercayaan]].

## Section 2 — Tiga Cara Berpikir tentang Estimation

### 2.1 Method of Moments

Idea:

> Cocokkan moments yang terlihat pada data dengan moments yang dimiliki model.

```text
theoretical moments
        =
sample moments
        ↓
solve unknown parameters
```

### 2.2 Maximum Likelihood

Idea:

> Untuk observed sample, cari parameter value yang membuat data tersebut paling “compatible” dengan model dalam arti memaksimalkan likelihood.

```text
model density/pmf
        ↓ iid product
likelihood L(theta)
        ↓
maximize over parameter domain
        ↓
MLE
```

### 2.3 Bayesian Estimation

Idea:

> Parameter diperlakukan sebagai random variable dengan prior distribution; data mengubah prior menjadi posterior.

```text
prior information
      ×
sample evidence
      ↓
posterior distribution
      ↓
Bayesian estimate / probability statement
```

## Section 3 — Method of Moments (MoM)

### 3.1 Sample Moments

Miller mendefinisikan $k$th sample moment sebagai

$$
\boxed{
m_k'
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^k
}
$$

untuk estimator form, atau setelah data diamati

$$
m_k'
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^k.
$$

Population moment ke-$k$ about the origin adalah

$$
\mu_k'=E[X^k].
$$

Jika model memiliki $r$ unknown parameters

$$
\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r,
$$

method of moments menggunakan $r$ equations:

$$
\boxed{
m_k'=\mu_k',
\qquad
k=1,\ldots,r
}
$$

kemudian menyelesaikannya terhadap unknown parameters.

> [!TIP] MoM Workflow
> **Jumlah unknown parameters = jumlah independent moment equations yang diperlukan.**

### 3.2 One-Parameter Case

Jika hanya ada satu unknown parameter $\theta$, sering kali first moment cukup:

$$
m_1'=\bar X=E_\theta[X].
$$

Solve:

$$
\bar X=g(\theta)
$$

sehingga

$$
\boxed{
\hat\theta_{\text{MoM}}=g^{-1}(\bar X)
}.
$$

### 3.3 Parameter Domain Check

Hasil algebra belum tentu automatically valid.

Jika parameter mempunyai domain

$$
\theta>0,
$$

tetapi expression MoM dapat menghasilkan nilai nonpositive untuk sample tertentu, ini harus dikenali sebagai domain issue.

Mental rule:

> **Solve → lalu check estimator terhadap parameter space.**

## Section 4 — Textbook Example: Uniform Population dengan Satu Unknown Parameter

Miller menggunakan continuous uniform population pada interval dengan endpoint $\alpha$ dan known $\beta=1$.

Untuk uniform $(\alpha,\beta)$,

$$
E[X]
=
\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}.
$$

Dengan

$$
\beta=1,
$$

maka

$$
\mu_1'
=
\frac{\alpha+1}{2}.
$$

Sample first moment:

$$
m_1'=\bar X.
$$

Set equal:

$$
\bar X
=
\frac{\alpha+1}{2}.
$$

Solve:

$$
2\bar X=\alpha+1,
$$

sehingga

$$
\boxed{
\hat\alpha_{\text{MoM}}
=
2\bar X-1
}.
$$

### Exam Lesson

MoM tidak dimulai dari likelihood. Cukup:

1. identify theoretical moment;
2. equate to corresponding sample moment;
3. solve.

## Section 5 — Textbook Example: Gamma dengan Dua Unknown Parameters

> [!NOTE] Parameterization
> Miller menggunakan Gamma parameterization dengan
>
> $$
> E[X]=\alpha\beta
> $$
>
> dan
>
> $$
> E[X^2]
> =
> \alpha(\alpha+1)\beta^2.
> $$
>
> Jadi $\beta$ adalah **scale parameter** pada contoh ini, bukan rate.

Unknowns:

$$
\alpha,\beta.
$$

Karena ada dua unknown parameters, gunakan dua moment equations:

$$
m_1'=\alpha\beta,
$$

$$
m_2'
=
\alpha(\alpha+1)\beta^2.
$$

Dari first equation,

$$
\beta=\frac{m_1'}{\alpha}.
$$

Substitute ke second equation:

$$
m_2'
=
\alpha(\alpha+1)
\left(
\frac{m_1'}{\alpha}
\right)^2.
$$

$$
=
\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}(m_1')^2.
$$

Maka

$$
\alpha m_2'
=
(\alpha+1)(m_1')^2,
$$

$$
\alpha
\left[
m_2'-(m_1')^2
\right]
=
(m_1')^2.
$$

Sehingga

$$
\boxed{
\hat\alpha_{\text{MoM}}
=
\frac{(m_1')^2}
{m_2'-(m_1')^2}
}
$$

dan

$$
\boxed{
\hat\beta_{\text{MoM}}
=
\frac{m_2'-(m_1')^2}
{m_1'}
}.
$$

Karena

$$
m_1'=\bar X
$$

dan

$$
m_2'-(m_1')^2
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2,
$$

Miller menulis equivalent forms

$$
\boxed{
\hat\alpha
=
\frac{n\bar X^2}
{\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2}
}
$$

dan

$$
\boxed{
\hat\beta
=
\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2}
{n\bar X}
}.
$$

> [!DANGER] Sample-Variance Denominator Trap
> MoM menggunakan raw sample moments dengan denominator $n$. Jangan otomatis mengganti
>
> $$
> \frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2
> $$
>
> dengan unbiased sample variance
>
> $$
> S^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2.
> $$
>
> Mereka bukan object yang sama dalam derivasi MoM.

## Section 6 — Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

### 6.1 Likelihood dari Random Sample

Misalkan

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n
$$

adalah random sample dari model

$$
f(x;\theta).
$$

Karena iid,

$$
f(x_1,\ldots,x_n;\theta)
=
\prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i;\theta).
$$

Untuk observed sample $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, expression yang sama dilihat sebagai function of $\theta$:

$$
\boxed{
L(\theta;x_1,\ldots,x_n)
=
\prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i;\theta)
}.
$$

MLE adalah parameter value yang memaksimalkan likelihood atas parameter space $\Theta$:

$$
\boxed{
\hat\theta_{\text{MLE}}
=
\arg\max_{\theta\in\Theta}
L(\theta)
}.
$$

> [!IMPORTANT] Likelihood Is Not a PDF of $\theta$
> Dalam frequentist MLE, $\theta$ bukan random variable.  
> $L(\theta)$ adalah function of parameter untuk **observed data fixed**.
>
> Jangan meminta
>
> $$
> \int L(\theta)\,d\theta=1.
> $$
>
> Normalization sebagai density parameter hanya muncul jika Bayesian model memperkenalkan prior/posterior.

---

## Section 7 — Log-Likelihood

Karena logarithm strictly increasing,

$$
\arg\max_\theta L(\theta)
=
\arg\max_\theta \log L(\theta).
$$

Define

$$
\boxed{
\ell(\theta)=\log L(\theta)
}.
$$

Product berubah menjadi sum:

$$
\ell(\theta)
=
\sum_{i=1}^n\log f(x_i;\theta).
$$

Ini biasanya jauh lebih mudah didifferentiate.

### Interior Stationary-Point Workflow

Jika maximizer berada di interior parameter domain:

$$
\frac{d}{d\theta}\ell(\theta)=0.
$$

Kemudian check:

- second derivative;
- monotonicity;
- endpoints/boundaries;
- support constraints.

> [!DANGER] Derivative-Zero Trap
> Menemukan root dari score equation belum membuktikan root itu MLE.
>
> MLE adalah **global maximizer pada valid parameter domain**.

## Section 8 — MLE Example: Bernoulli/Binomial Parameter

Misalkan

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n
\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\theta),
\qquad
0\le\theta\le1.
$$

PMF:

$$
f(x_i;\theta)
=
\theta^{x_i}(1-\theta)^{1-x_i},
\qquad
x_i\in\{0,1\}.
$$

Likelihood:

$$
L(\theta)
=
\prod_{i=1}^n
\theta^{x_i}(1-\theta)^{1-x_i}.
$$

Collect exponents:

$$
L(\theta)
=
\theta^{\sum x_i}
(1-\theta)^{n-\sum x_i}.
$$

Log-likelihood:

$$
\ell(\theta)
=
\left(\sum x_i\right)\log\theta
+
\left(n-\sum x_i\right)\log(1-\theta).
$$

Differentiate:

$$
\ell'(\theta)
=
\frac{\sum x_i}{\theta}
-
\frac{n-\sum x_i}{1-\theta}.
$$

Set equal to zero:

$$
\frac{\sum x_i}{\theta}
=
\frac{n-\sum x_i}{1-\theta}.
$$

Cross-multiply:

$$
(1-\theta)\sum x_i
=
\theta
\left(
n-\sum x_i
\right).
$$

Thus

$$
\sum x_i=n\theta,
$$

sehingga

$$
\boxed{
\hat\theta_{\text{MLE}}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nX_i
=
\bar X
}.
$$

For Bernoulli data, $\bar X$ adalah sample proportion of successes.

### Boundary Check

Jika semua observations $0$,

$$
\sum x_i=0,
$$

likelihood maximized pada

$$
\hat\theta=0.
$$

Jika semua observations $1$,

$$
\hat\theta=1.
$$

Jadi formula $\bar X$ tetap consistent dengan boundary cases.

## Section 9 — MLE Example: Normal Population, Unknown $\mu$ dan $\sigma^2$

Miller considers

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n
\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}
N(\mu,\sigma^2).
$$

Likelihood:

$$
L(\mu,\sigma^2)
=
\left(
\frac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
\right)^n
\exp\left[
-\frac1{2\sigma^2}
\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-\mu)^2
\right].
$$

Log-likelihood:

$$
\ell(\mu,\sigma^2)
=
-n\log\sigma
-\frac n2\log(2\pi)
-\frac1{2\sigma^2}
\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-\mu)^2.
$$

Partial derivative with respect to $\mu$:

$$
\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\mu}
=
\frac1{\sigma^2}
\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-\mu).
$$

Set to zero:

$$
\sum x_i-n\mu=0.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{
\hat\mu_{\text{MLE}}=\bar X
}.
$$

For $\sigma^2$,

$$
\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\sigma^2}
=
-\frac n{2\sigma^2}
+
\frac1{2\sigma^4}
\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-\mu)^2.
$$

Set equal to zero and substitute $\mu=\bar x$:

$$
-n\sigma^2
+
\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-\bar x)^2
=
0.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
\hat\sigma_{\text{MLE}}^2
=
\frac1n
\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2
}.
$$

> [!DANGER] $n$ vs $n-1$ Trap
> MLE untuk Normal variance adalah
>
> $$
> \frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2,
> $$
>
> bukan
>
> $$
> S^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2.
> $$
>
> Yang kedua dipilih karena unbiasedness; unbiasedness adalah property issue untuk [[4.6 Sifat-Sifat Estimator]], bukan definisi MLE.

## Section 10 — MLE when Support Depends on Parameter

Derivative method sangat berbahaya jika $\theta$ muncul di **support**.

Miller memberi Uniform population dengan density proportional to

$$
\frac1\beta
$$

pada interval whose upper endpoint is $\beta$.

Untuk entire sample to be possible, harus

$$
\beta\ge x_{(n)},
$$

where

$$
x_{(n)}=\max(X_1,\ldots,X_n).
$$

Di valid region,

$$
L(\beta)\propto\beta^{-n}.
$$

Karena likelihood decreases as $\beta$ increases, maximum diperoleh pada smallest allowed value:

$$
\boxed{
\hat\beta_{\text{MLE}}
=
X_{(n)}
}.
$$

### Mental Rule

> **Jika support depends on parameter, tulis support constraint sebelum differentiate.**

Typical structure:

$$
L(\theta)
=
g(\theta)\,
I\{\theta\in A(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\}.
$$

Constraint set $A$ dapat menentukan MLE langsung.

---

## Section 11 — MLE Need Not Be Unique

Miller memberikan shifted Uniform model

$$
f(x;\theta)
=
1
$$

untuk

$$
\theta-\frac12<x<\theta+\frac12
$$

dan zero elsewhere.

For observed sample, every point must satisfy

$$
\theta-\frac12<x_i<\theta+\frac12.
$$

Equivalent sample constraints lead to an interval of $\theta$ values that all yield the same maximum likelihood.

Lesson:

$$
\boxed{
\text{MLE need not be unique.}
}
$$

Ini lagi-lagi menunjukkan bahwa optimization harus melihat **whole likelihood + support**, bukan hanya solve derivative equation.

## Section 12 — Invariance Property of MLE

Miller notes the invariance property:

Jika

$$
\hat\theta
$$

is an MLE of $\theta$ dan $g$ continuous, maka

$$
\boxed{
g(\hat\theta)
}
$$

is an MLE of

$$
g(\theta).
$$

Contoh dari Normal model:

$$
\hat\sigma^2
=
\frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2.
$$

Then MLE of $\sigma$ is

$$
\boxed{
\hat\sigma
=
\sqrt{
\frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2
}
}.
$$

> [!TIP] Transformation Shortcut
> Jika MLE parameter dasar sudah diketahui dan target adalah simple continuous transformation, gunakan invariance daripada mengoptimalkan likelihood dari awal.

## Section 13 — Method of Moments vs MLE

| Aspect | Method of Moments | Maximum Likelihood |
|---|---|---|
| Main Object | Moments | Joint likelihood |
| Starts From | $E[X^k]$ | PMF/PDF |
| Number of equations | Usually number of parameters | Optimization dimension |
| Support dependence | Often indirect | Can be decisive |
| Calculus needed | Often algebra | Often derivatives/logs |
| Parameter-domain check | Required | Essential |
| Gives unique estimator? | Not guaranteed by method alone | Not necessarily |
| “Best” automatically? | No | No |
| Typical speed | Fast if moments simple | Fast if likelihood factorizes cleanly |

> [!IMPORTANT] No Automatic Superiority
> Silabus asks students to **determine estimators using the methods**. It does not justify saying “MLE is always better than MoM.” Estimator quality requires a stated criterion, addressed in [[4.6 Sifat-Sifat Estimator]].

## Section 14 — Bayesian Estimation: Different Statistical View

Frequentist sections treat

$$
\theta
$$

as an unknown constant.

Miller explicitly changes perspective for Bayesian estimation:

> parameter is treated as a random variable with a **prior distribution**.

Let prior density be

$$
h(\theta).
$$

Suppose sample evidence is summarized by statistic

$$
W=u(X_1,\ldots,X_n).
$$

Let

$$
f(w\mid\theta)
$$

be sampling distribution of $W$ given $\theta$.

The joint density is

$$
f(\theta,w)
=
h(\theta)f(w\mid\theta).
$$

Therefore posterior density:

$$
\boxed{
\phi(\theta\mid w)
=
\frac{
h(\theta)f(w\mid\theta)
}{
g(w)
}
}
$$

where

$$
g(w)
=
\int h(\theta)f(w\mid\theta)\,d\theta
$$

for a continuous parameter.

Equivalent proportional form:

$$
\boxed{
\phi(\theta\mid w)
\propto
h(\theta)f(w\mid\theta)
}.
$$

This is continuous Bayes theorem.

### Four Objects Must Be Distinguished

| Object | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $h(\theta)$ | Prior distribution |
| $f(w\mid\theta)$ | Sampling model / data evidence |
| $h(\theta)f(w\mid\theta)$ | Unnormalized posterior |
| $\phi(\theta\mid w)$ | Normalized posterior |

> [!DANGER] Frequentist-vs-Bayesian Likelihood Trap
> In MLE:
>
> $$
> L(\theta)
> $$
>
> is **not** a probability distribution over $\theta$.
>
> In Bayesian estimation:
>
> prior $\times$ likelihood-like evidence is normalized into an actual posterior distribution over $\theta$.

## Section 15 — Beta–Binomial Bayesian Updating

Miller's Theorem 5 gives a conjugate pair.

Suppose

$$
X\mid\Theta=\theta
\sim
\operatorname{Binomial}(n,\theta).
$$

Prior:

$$
\Theta\sim\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha,\beta).
$$

The binomial likelihood contribution in $\theta$ is

$$
f(x\mid\theta)
\propto
\theta^x(1-\theta)^{n-x}.
$$

Beta prior kernel:

$$
h(\theta)
\propto
\theta^{\alpha-1}(1-\theta)^{\beta-1},
\qquad
0<\theta<1.
$$

Multiply:

$$
h(\theta)f(x\mid\theta)
\propto
\theta^{x+\alpha-1}
(1-\theta)^{n-x+\beta-1}.
$$

This is Beta kernel with updated parameters

$$
\boxed{
\Theta\mid X=x
\sim
\operatorname{Beta}
(
x+\alpha,\,
n-x+\beta
)
}.
$$

### Posterior Mean under Quadratic Loss

Miller states that under quadratic loss, posterior mean minimizes Bayes risk.

For Beta posterior,

$$
\boxed{
E[\Theta\mid X=x]
=
\frac{x+\alpha}
{n+\alpha+\beta}
}.
$$

This is the Bayesian point estimator in the source under the stated setup/loss.

---

## Section 16 — Textbook Bayesian Example: Binomial Probability

Miller gives:

$$
x=42,
\qquad
n=120,
\qquad
\alpha=\beta=40.
$$

Posterior:

$$
\Theta\mid X=42
\sim
\operatorname{Beta}(82,118).
$$

Posterior mean:

$$
E[\Theta\mid X=42]
=
\frac{42+40}
{120+40+40}.
$$

$$
=
\frac{82}{200}
=
\boxed{0.41}.
$$

Data-only sample proportion is

$$
\frac{x}{n}
=
\frac{42}{120}
=
0.35.
$$

Prior mean is

$$
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}
=
\frac{40}{80}
=
0.50.
$$

The Bayesian estimate

$$
0.41
$$

lies between prior mean $0.50$ and data-only estimate $0.35$, reflecting combination of both information sources.

### Weighted-Average View

Posterior mean can be rewritten as

$$
\frac{x+\alpha}{n+\alpha+\beta}
=
\frac{n}{n+\alpha+\beta}
\left(
\frac xn
\right)
+
\frac{\alpha+\beta}{n+\alpha+\beta}
\left(
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}
\right).
$$

Thus it is a weighted combination of:

- sample proportion;
- prior mean.

> [!TIP] Fast Interpretation
> Larger $n$ gives more weight to data. Larger $\alpha+\beta$ gives more weight to the prior.

## Section 17 — Normal–Normal Bayesian Updating

Miller's Theorem 6 considers:

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n
\mid M=\mu
\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}
N(\mu,\sigma^2),
$$

with known

$$
\sigma^2.
$$

Prior on population mean:

$$
M\sim
N(\mu_0,\sigma_0^2).
$$

Because

$$
\bar X\mid M=\mu
\sim
N\left(
\mu,\frac{\sigma^2}{n}
\right),
$$

posterior is again Normal:

$$
\boxed{
M\mid\bar X=\bar x
\sim
N(\mu_1,\sigma_1^2)
}
$$

with

$$
\boxed{
\mu_1
=
\frac{
n\bar x\,\sigma_0^2
+
\mu_0\sigma^2
}{
n\sigma_0^2+\sigma^2
}
}
$$

and precision form

$$
\boxed{
\frac1{\sigma_1^2}
=
\frac n{\sigma^2}
+
\frac1{\sigma_0^2}
}.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\sigma_1^2
=
\frac1{
n/\sigma^2+1/\sigma_0^2
}.
$$

### Precision Interpretation

Define:

$$
\text{data precision}
=
\frac n{\sigma^2},
$$

$$
\text{prior precision}
=
\frac1{\sigma_0^2}.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
\text{posterior precision}
=
\text{data precision}
+
\text{prior precision}
}.
$$

Posterior mean is a precision-weighted average of sample mean and prior mean.

## Section 18 — Textbook Normal-Prior Example

Miller gives:

$$
\mu_0=738,
\qquad
\sigma_0=13.4,
$$

within-market weekly standard deviation

$$
\sigma=42.5,
$$

sample size

$$
n=10,
$$

and observed sample mean

$$
\bar x=692.
$$

Posterior mean:

$$
\mu_1
=
\frac{
10(692)(13.4)^2
+
738(42.5)^2
}{
10(13.4)^2+(42.5)^2
}
=
715.
$$

Posterior precision:

$$
\frac1{\sigma_1^2}
=
\frac{10}{(42.5)^2}
+
\frac1{(13.4)^2}
=
0.0111.
$$

Thus approximately

$$
\sigma_1^2=90.0,
\qquad
\sigma_1=9.5.
$$

To compute

$$
P(700<M<720\mid\bar X=692),
$$

standardize posterior endpoints:

$$
z_L
=
\frac{700-715}{9.5}
=
-1.58,
$$

$$
z_U
=
\frac{720-715}{9.5}
=
0.53.
$$

Using standard Normal areas as in Miller:

$$
P(700<M<720\mid\bar X=692)
=
0.4429+0.2019
=
\boxed{0.6448}.
$$

### Meaning

This probability statement is valid because **Bayesian posterior treats $M$ as random conditional on observed evidence**. This differs fundamentally from frequentist confidence-interval interpretation.

## Section 19 — How It Works

### 19.1 Method of Moments

```text
identify parameter(s)
        ↓
write theoretical moment(s)
        ↓
write corresponding sample moment(s)
        ↓
equate
        ↓
solve
        ↓
check parameter domain
```

### 19.2 MLE

```text
specify PMF/PDF + support
        ↓
write iid likelihood
        ↓
identify parameter domain
        ↓
log-likelihood if useful
        ↓
differentiate / compare boundaries
        ↓
candidate maximizer
        ↓
verify global maximum + support
```

### 19.3 Bayesian

```text
specify prior
        ↓
specify sampling model
        ↓
prior × likelihood
        ↓
recognize posterior kernel / normalize
        ↓
posterior distribution
        ↓
posterior mean / probability statement as requested
```

> [!TIP] Cara Berpikir Cepat
> 1. **MoM:** jumlah unknown = jumlah moments.
> 2. **MLE:** buang factors constant in $\theta$ when maximizing.
> 3. **MLE:** use log if exponentials/products appear.
> 4. **MLE with parameterized support:** inspect order statistics before calculus.
> 5. **Bayesian conjugacy:** multiply kernels; constants independent of $\theta$ can be postponed until normalization.
> 6. Always declare parameterization when Gamma/Beta-like forms are involved.

## Section 20 — Worked Examples & Exam Cases

### Case A — Fundamental: MoM untuk Poisson

Suppose

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n
\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}
\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda).
$$

Population first moment:

$$
E[X]=\lambda.
$$

Sample first moment:

$$
m_1'=\bar X.
$$

Set equal:

$$
\bar X=\lambda.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\hat\lambda_{\text{MoM}}=\bar X
}.
$$

**Check:** since Poisson parameter requires $\lambda\ge0$ and $\bar X\ge0$, estimator respects domain.

---

### Case B — Exam-Typical: MLE untuk Exponential Mean/Scale Parameter

> [!NOTE] Parameterization
> Gunakan Exponential parameterization
>
> $$
> f(x;\theta)
> =
> \frac1\theta e^{-x/\theta},
> \qquad x>0,\quad\theta>0,
> $$
>
> sehingga
>
> $$
> E[X]=\theta.
> $$

For random sample,

$$
L(\theta)
=
\theta^{-n}
\exp\left(
-\frac1\theta
\sum_{i=1}^n x_i
\right).
$$

Log-likelihood:

$$
\ell(\theta)
=
-n\log\theta
-
\frac1\theta
\sum x_i.
$$

Differentiate:

$$
\ell'(\theta)
=
-\frac n\theta
+
\frac{\sum x_i}{\theta^2}.
$$

Set zero:

$$
-n\theta+\sum x_i=0.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{
\hat\theta_{\text{MLE}}
=
\bar X
}.
$$

Second derivative at $\theta=\bar x$ is negative, confirming local maximum; likelihood also tends appropriately at parameter boundaries for positive sample data.

---

### Case C — Challenging: Uniform Support Determines MLE

Suppose

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n
\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}
\operatorname{Uniform}(0,\theta),
\qquad
\theta>0.
$$

PDF:

$$
f(x;\theta)
=
\frac1\theta,
\qquad
0<x<\theta.
$$

Likelihood:

$$
L(\theta)
=
\theta^{-n}
I\{\theta\ge X_{(n)}\}.
$$

On valid region

$$
\theta\ge X_{(n)},
$$

$\theta^{-n}$ strictly decreases as $\theta$ increases.

Therefore maximum occurs at smallest permitted value:

$$
\boxed{
\hat\theta_{\text{MLE}}
=
X_{(n)}
}.
$$

**Exam trap:** differentiating $\theta^{-n}$ gives no interior stationary point; the MLE is a **boundary value determined by support**.

---

### Case D — Bayesian Beta–Binomial

Prior:

$$
\Theta\sim\operatorname{Beta}(4,6).
$$

Data:

$$
x=12
$$

successes from

$$
n=20
$$

Bernoulli trials.

Posterior parameters:

$$
\alpha_{\text{post}}
=
4+12
=
16,
$$

$$
\beta_{\text{post}}
=
6+(20-12)
=
14.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{
\Theta\mid X=12
\sim
\operatorname{Beta}(16,14)
}.
$$

Posterior mean under quadratic loss:

$$
E[\Theta\mid X=12]
=
\frac{16}{30}
=
\boxed{0.5333}.
$$

Compare:

$$
\text{prior mean}
=
\frac4{10}
=
0.4,
$$

$$
\text{sample proportion}
=
\frac{12}{20}
=
0.6.
$$

Posterior estimate lies between them, as expected.

## Section 21 — Verification Checklist

### For Method of Moments

- Did I use raw sample moments?
- Number of equations = number of unknown parameters?
- Are theoretical moments correct for the declared parameterization?
- Does solution lie in parameter domain?

### For MLE

- Is likelihood built from iid product?
- Did I include support indicators/constraints?
- Did I remove only factors constant in parameter?
- Is log-likelihood transformation valid?
- Is stationary point actually a maximum?
- Did I check boundaries?
- Could MLE be nonunique?

### For Bayesian Estimation

- Is prior properly identified?
- Is likelihood/data model conditioned on parameter?
- Did I multiply prior and likelihood kernels correctly?
- Is posterior normalized or recognized as a known normalized family?
- Did I update both success and failure counts for Beta–Binomial?
- Does posterior probability integrate to $1$?
- If using posterior mean, is the loss/criterion supported by source?

## Section 22 — Exam Traps & Misconceptions

### 22.1 Estimator vs Estimate Trap

Before data:

$$
\hat\theta=T(X_1,\ldots,X_n)
$$

is random.

After data:

$$
T(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
$$

is numerical.

Do not swap notation carelessly.

---

### 22.2 Likelihood-as-Probability Trap

**Wrong:**

> $L(\theta)$ is the probability that $\theta$ is true.

**Correct:**

> For fixed observed data, likelihood compares parameter values through the model.

Only Bayesian posterior creates a probability distribution on the parameter.

---

### 22.3 Ignoring Support Trap

If

$$
f(x;\theta)
$$

has support depending on $\theta$, the likelihood may be zero for large parts of parameter space.

Order statistics often become central.

---

### 22.4 Stationary Point Trap

$$
\ell'(\theta)=0
$$

creates a candidate, not proof of global maximum.

---

### 22.5 $n$ vs $n-1$ Trap

For Normal variance MLE:

$$
\hat\sigma^2_{\text{MLE}}
=
\frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2.
$$

Do not automatically use unbiased $S^2$.

---

### 22.6 MoM Central-vs-Raw Moment Trap

Method of moments as stated by Miller uses

$$
m_k'
=
\frac1n\sum X_i^k,
$$

i.e. moments about origin. Do not replace second raw moment with sample variance unless algebra explicitly transforms it.

---

### 22.7 Parameterization Trap

Gamma, Exponential, and related distributions can use different rate/scale conventions.

Always write PDF or mean relation before deriving estimator.

---

### 22.8 Bayesian Prior-Posterior Trap

Prior:

$$
h(\theta)
$$

is before sample evidence.

Posterior:

$$
\phi(\theta\mid x)
$$

is after evidence.

Do not reverse them.

---

### 22.9 Beta–Binomial Update Trap

Correct:

$$
\alpha_{\text{post}}=\alpha+x,
$$

$$
\beta_{\text{post}}=\beta+n-x.
$$

Failures update $\beta$; do not add $n$ directly.

---

### 22.10 MLE Automatically Unbiased Trap

False. Miller explicitly notes that MLEs need not be unbiased.

Estimator properties must be checked separately.

## Section 23 — Formula Map

| Concept | Formula | Key Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Estimator | $\hat\theta=T(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ | statistic used for parameter |
| Estimate | $T(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ | observed data |
| $k$th sample raw moment | $m_k'=\frac1n\sum X_i^k$ | MoM |
| MoM system | $m_k'=\mu_k'$, $k=1,\ldots,r$ | $r$ parameters |
| Likelihood | $L(\theta)=\prod_i f(x_i;\theta)$ | iid random sample |
| Log-likelihood | $\ell(\theta)=\sum_i\log f(x_i;\theta)$ | same maximizer as $L$ |
| MLE | $\hat\theta=\arg\max_{\theta\in\Theta}L(\theta)$ | domain/support required |
| Bernoulli MLE | $\hat\theta=\bar X$ | $\theta\in[0,1]$ |
| Normal mean MLE | $\hat\mu=\bar X$ | Normal iid |
| Normal variance MLE | $\hat\sigma^2=\frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2$ | Normal iid |
| Uniform $(0,\theta)$ MLE | $\hat\theta=X_{(n)}$ | support depends on $\theta$ |
| MLE invariance | MLE of $g(\theta)$ is $g(\hat\theta)$ | continuous $g$ per Miller |
| Posterior | $\phi(\theta\mid w)=h(\theta)f(w\mid\theta)/g(w)$ | Bayesian model |
| Bayesian kernel | posterior $\propto$ prior $\times$ likelihood | normalize over parameter support |
| Beta–Binomial posterior | $\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha+x,\beta+n-x)$ | Beta prior, Binomial data |
| Beta–Binomial posterior mean | $(x+\alpha)/(n+\alpha+\beta)$ | quadratic-loss Bayes estimate |
| Normal prior posterior precision | $1/\sigma_1^2=n/\sigma^2+1/\sigma_0^2$ | known sampling variance |
| Normal posterior mean | $\mu_1=\frac{n\bar x\sigma_0^2+\mu_0\sigma^2}{n\sigma_0^2+\sigma^2}$ | Normal prior + Normal sampling mean |
