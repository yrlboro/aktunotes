## Section 1 — Big Picture: Dari Point Estimate ke Interval Estimate

Point estimate memberi satu angka $\hat\theta$, tetapi angka tunggal tidak memberi informasi langsung tentang sampling uncertainty. Confidence interval menambahkan range:

$$
\boxed{L(X_1,\ldots,X_n)<\theta<U(X_1,\ldots,X_n)}
$$

dengan property:

$$
\boxed{P(L<\theta<U)=1-\alpha.}
$$

Setelah data diamati, $L$ dan $U$ menjadi angka. Miller menyebut interval tersebut sebagai

$$
\boxed{(1-\alpha)100\%\text{ confidence interval}.}
$$

Typical values:

- $90\%\Rightarrow \alpha=0.10$;
- $95\%\Rightarrow \alpha=0.05$;
- $99\%\Rightarrow \alpha=0.01$.

Untuk symmetric two-sided interval, masing-masing tail adalah $\alpha/2$.

---

## Section 2 — Interpretation Trap: Confidence Bukan Posterior Probability

Sebelum sample diamati, endpoints interval adalah random:

$$
P(L<\theta<U)=1-\alpha.
$$

Setelah data diamati, parameter dan interval endpoints fixed. Jadi frequentist interpretation yang benar:

> **Procedure yang digunakan menghasilkan intervals yang mencakup true parameter dalam proporsi $1-\alpha$ dari repeated samples.**

Secara informal kita boleh berkata, “kita 95% confident bahwa interval ini mencakup parameter.” Tetapi jangan menginterpretasikan fixed observed interval sebagai posterior probability statement tentang fixed parameter.

> [!DANGER] Probability Interpretation Trap
> Frequentist $95\%$ CI bukan berarti $P(\theta\in[L,U]\mid\text{data})=0.95$.

---

## Section 3 — Pivotal Method

A **pivot** melibatkan sample dan unknown parameter, tetapi memiliki distribution yang tidak bergantung pada unknown parameter yang sedang diisolasi. Contoh:

$$
Z=\frac{\bar X-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt n}\sim N(0,1).
$$

Workflow:

```text
sampling distribution
      ↓
construct pivot
      ↓
place pivot in central probability 1-α
      ↓
solve inequality for parameter
      ↓
plug observed statistics
      ↓
confidence interval
```

Mental rule:

> **Target parameter → assumptions → pivot → critical value → solve → interval → sanity check**

## Section 4 — CI untuk Satu Mean: $\sigma$ Known

Untuk random sample dari Normal population dengan $\sigma$ known:

$$
Z=\frac{\bar X-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt n}\sim N(0,1).
$$

Maka

$$
\boxed{\bar x\pm z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}.}
$$

Margin of error:

$$
\boxed{E=z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}.}
$$

### Textbook Example

Given $n=20$, $\bar x=64.3$, $\sigma=15$, dan $z_{0.025}=1.96$:

$$
E=1.96\frac{15}{\sqrt{20}}\approx6.57,
$$

sehingga

$$
\boxed{57.7<\mu<70.9.}
$$

### Maximum Error

Miller example: $n=150$, $\sigma=6.2$, confidence $99\%$, $z_{0.005}=2.575$:

$$
E=2.575\frac{6.2}{\sqrt{150}}\approx\boxed{1.30}.
$$

---

## Section 5 — CI untuk Satu Mean: $\sigma$ Unknown

Untuk random sample dari **Normal population**, ketika $\sigma$ unknown:

$$
T=\frac{\bar X-\mu}{S/\sqrt n}\sim t_{n-1}.
$$

CI:

$$
\boxed{\bar x\pm t_{\alpha/2,n-1}\frac{s}{\sqrt n}.}
$$

Assumptions:

- random sample;
- population Normal untuk exact small-sample result;
- $\sigma$ unknown;
- $df=n-1$.

### Textbook Example

Given $n=12$, $\bar x=66.3$, $s=8.4$, $t_{0.025,11}=2.201$:

$$
\boxed{61.0<\mu<71.6.}
$$

> [!DANGER] Z-vs-t Trap
> Unknown $\sigma$ + exact small Normal sample menggunakan $t$, bukan sekadar mengganti $\sigma$ dengan $s$ pada formula $Z$.

---

## Section 6 — Large-Sample Mean Approximation

Berdasarkan CLT, Miller menggunakan Normal approximation untuk non-Normal populations ketika sample size besar. `[TEXTBOOK CONTEXT]` Miller menggunakan guideline praktis $n\ge30$.

Jika $\sigma$ unknown dan $n$ besar, Miller menggunakan approximate interval:

$$
\boxed{\bar x\pm z_{\alpha/2}\frac{s}{\sqrt n}.}
$$

Distinction:

- Normal + known $\sigma$ → exact $Z$;
- Normal + unknown $\sigma$ → exact $t$;
- non-Normal large sample → CLT-based approximation.

---

## Section 7 — CI untuk Difference of Two Means

Target:

$$
\mu_1-\mu_2.
$$

### Known Population Variances

Untuk independent samples:

$$
\boxed{(\bar x_1-\bar x_2)\pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}}.}
$$

> [!DANGER] Variance-of-Difference Trap
> Walaupun means dikurangkan, independent variances tetap **ditambahkan**.

### Large Independent Samples, Variances Unknown

Approximate interval:

$$
\boxed{(\bar x_1-\bar x_2)\pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}.}
$$

---

## Section 8 — Small Independent Normal Samples, Equal Unknown Variances

Assume

$$
\sigma_1^2=\sigma_2^2=\sigma^2.
$$

Pooled variance:

$$
\boxed{S_p^2=\frac{(n_1-1)S_1^2+(n_2-1)S_2^2}{n_1+n_2-2}.}
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{(\bar x_1-\bar x_2)\pm t_{\alpha/2,n_1+n_2-2}s_p\sqrt{\frac1{n_1}+\frac1{n_2}}.}
$$

Exact assumptions:

1. independent samples;
2. Normal populations;
3. equal population variances.

Degrees of freedom:

$$
\boxed{n_1+n_2-2.}
$$

### Textbook Nicotine Example

Given $n_1=10$, $\bar x_1=3.1$, $s_1=0.5$, $n_2=8$, $\bar x_2=2.7$, $s_2=0.7$, Miller obtains $s_p\approx0.596$ and

$$
\boxed{-0.20<\mu_1-\mu_2<1.00.}
$$

Because $0$ lies in the interval, zero difference remains compatible with the data.

---

## Section 9 — CI untuk Satu Proportion

Let

$$
X\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,\theta),\qquad \hat\theta=\frac Xn.
$$

For large $n$:

$$
\boxed{\hat\theta\pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat\theta(1-\hat\theta)}{n}}.}
$$

This is an **approximate** Normal interval.

### Textbook Vaccine Example

If $x=136$ of $n=400$ persons experience discomfort:

$$
\hat\theta=0.34.
$$

A $95\%$ interval gives:

$$
\boxed{0.294<\theta<0.386.}
$$

Margin of error:

$$
\boxed{E=z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat\theta(1-\hat\theta)}{n}}.}
$$

---

## Section 10 — CI untuk Difference of Two Proportions

For two independent large Binomial samples:

$$
\hat\theta_1=\frac{X_1}{n_1},\qquad \hat\theta_2=\frac{X_2}{n_2}.
$$

CI:

$$
\boxed{(\hat\theta_1-\hat\theta_2)\pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat\theta_1(1-\hat\theta_1)}{n_1}+\frac{\hat\theta_2(1-\hat\theta_2)}{n_2}}.}
$$

### Textbook Voter Example

Male voters: $132/200=0.66$; female voters: $90/150=0.60$; confidence $99\%$.

Miller obtains:

$$
\boxed{-0.074<\theta_1-\theta_2<0.194.}
$$

Since $0$ lies in the interval, zero population difference remains plausible.

> [!TIP] Difference Benchmark
> Untuk parameter berbentuk difference, $0$ adalah natural “no difference” benchmark.

---

## Section 11 — CI untuk Variance

Untuk random sample dari a **Normal population**:

$$
\boxed{\frac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma^2}\sim\chi^2_{n-1}.}
$$

Using Miller's upper-tail critical-value notation:

$$
\boxed{\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{\alpha/2,n-1}}<\sigma^2<\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{1-\alpha/2,n-1}}.}
$$

This interval is asymmetric because the Chi-square distribution is asymmetric.

### Textbook Engine Example

Given $n=16$, $s=2.2$, for $99\%$ confidence Miller obtains:

$$
\boxed{2.21<\sigma^2<15.78.}
$$

Taking square roots:

$$
\boxed{1.49<\sigma<3.97.}
$$

> [!ASSUMPTION]
> Exact variance CI requires a **Normal population**.

> [!IMPORTANT] Critical-Value Convention
> Miller uses upper-tail $\chi^2$ notation. Software often uses lower-tail quantiles, so subscripts may appear reversed unless translated carefully.

---

## Section 12 — CI untuk Ratio of Two Variances

For two independent random samples from Normal populations:

$$
\frac{S_1^2/\sigma_1^2}{S_2^2/\sigma_2^2}\sim F_{n_1-1,n_2-1}.
$$

Miller's interval:

$$
\boxed{\frac{s_1^2}{s_2^2}\frac1{f_{\alpha/2,n_1-1,n_2-1}}<\frac{\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_2^2}<\frac{s_1^2}{s_2^2}f_{\alpha/2,n_2-1,n_1-1}.}
$$

Degrees of freedom:

$$
\nu_1=n_1-1,\qquad \nu_2=n_2-1.
$$

CI for $\sigma_1/\sigma_2$ is obtained by taking square roots of both endpoints.

### Textbook Ratio Example

Given $s_1=0.5$, $s_2=0.7$, $n_1=10$, $n_2=8$, Miller obtains a $98\%$ CI:

$$
\boxed{0.076<\frac{\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_2^2}<2.862.}
$$

Since $1$ lies in the interval, equal variances remain plausible.

> [!TIP] Ratio Benchmark
> Untuk variance ratio, $1$ adalah natural equality benchmark.

## Section 13 — Confidence-Interval Recognition Table

| Target Parameter | Situation | Critical Distribution | Formula Skeleton |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mu$ | $\sigma$ known | $Z$ | $\bar x\pm z\sigma/\sqrt n$ |
| $\mu$ | $\sigma$ unknown, Normal | $t_{n-1}$ | $\bar x\pm ts/\sqrt n$ |
| $\mu_1-\mu_2$ | independent, variances known | $Z$ | difference $\pm z\times SE$ |
| $\mu_1-\mu_2$ | large independent samples | approximate $Z$ | use $s_1,s_2$ in SE |
| $\mu_1-\mu_2$ | small Normal, equal unknown variances | pooled $t$ | difference $\pm t s_p\sqrt{1/n_1+1/n_2}$ |
| $\theta$ | large Binomial sample | approximate $Z$ | $\hat\theta\pm z\sqrt{\hat\theta(1-\hat\theta)/n}$ |
| $\theta_1-\theta_2$ | large independent Binomial samples | approximate $Z$ | difference $\pm z\times SE$ |
| $\sigma^2$ | Normal sample | $\chi^2$ | asymmetric reciprocal critical values |
| $\sigma_1^2/\sigma_2^2$ | independent Normal samples | $F$ | variance ratio × $F$ factors |

## Section 14 — Confidence Level, Width, and Sample Size

For known-$\sigma$ mean:

$$
E=z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{higher confidence}\Rightarrow\text{wider interval}}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\text{larger }n\Rightarrow\text{narrower interval}}.
$$

Larger variability also widens the interval.

### Sample Size from Desired Margin of Error

Solve

$$
E=z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}
$$

to obtain

$$
\boxed{n=\left(\frac{z_{\alpha/2}\sigma}{E}\right)^2.}
$$

If noninteger, round **up**.

---

## Section 15 — Exact vs Approximate Map

### Exact

- known-$\sigma$ mean from Normal population;
- unknown-$\sigma$ mean via $t$ from Normal population;
- pooled two-mean $t$ with independent Normal populations and equal variances;
- variance via $\chi^2$ from Normal population;
- variance ratio via $F$ from independent Normal populations.

### Approximate

- large-sample non-Normal mean via CLT;
- large-sample mean with unknown $\sigma$ replacing it by $s$;
- one-proportion CI via Normal approximation;
- difference-of-proportions CI via Normal approximation.

## Section 16 — Universal Exam Decision Framework

```text
1. What parameter?
     ↓
2. One sample or two?
     ↓
3. Mean / proportion / variance / variance ratio?
     ↓
4. What is known?
   - σ known?
   - σ unknown?
   - equal variances?
     ↓
5. Normal population or large sample?
     ↓
6. Choose Z / t / χ² / F
     ↓
7. Compute SE or pivot scale
     ↓
8. Critical value + df
     ↓
9. Build interval
     ↓
10. Interpret using benchmark 0 or 1 when relevant
```

## Section 17 — High-Yield Exam Traps

1. **$Z$ vs $t$:** known $\sigma$ → $Z$; unknown $\sigma$ + exact Normal sample → $t$.
2. **Degrees of freedom:** $n-1$, $n_1+n_2-2$, or $(n_1-1,n_2-1)$ depending on distribution.
3. **SD vs SE:** use $\sigma/\sqrt n$, not $\sigma$.
4. **Difference variance:** independent variances add.
5. **Pooling:** only with equal-variance assumption.
6. **Proportion CI:** approximate, not exact.
7. **$\chi^2$ notation:** check upper-tail vs lower-tail convention.
8. **$F$ inversion:** swap numerator/denominator df when ratio is inverted.
9. **Difference benchmark:** check $0$.
10. **Ratio benchmark:** check $1$.
11. **Confidence interpretation:** coverage procedure, not posterior probability.
12. **Margin vs width:** margin of error is half the symmetric CI width.
