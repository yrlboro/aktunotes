# Berk & DeMarzo — Chapter 17: Payout Policy

> [!ABSTRACT] Chapter in One View
> Chapter 17 membahas keputusan perusahaan setelah menghasilkan **free cash flow**: apakah cash tersebut harus **dibayarkan kepada shareholders** atau **ditahan di perusahaan**. Jika dibayarkan, dua mekanisme utamanya adalah **cash dividend** dan **share repurchase**.
>
> Berk & DeMarzo memulai dari benchmark **perfect capital markets**. Dalam kondisi ini, cara membayar shareholders tidak menciptakan value. Cash dividend menurunkan share price sebesar dividend ketika stock menjadi **ex-dividend**, sedangkan open-market repurchase mengurangi cash dan jumlah shares secara bersamaan sehingga share price tidak berubah. Dengan investment policy tetap, **Modigliani–Miller dividend irrelevance proposition** menyatakan payout method tidak memengaruhi initial share price.
>
> Dunia nyata tidak perfect. Taxes dapat membuat dividends kurang menarik daripada capital gains, sehingga repurchases memperoleh tax advantage bagi sebagian investors. Namun tax rates berbeda menurut investor, holding period, jurisdiction, dan account type; perbedaan tersebut menimbulkan **clientele effects** dan **dividend-capture behavior**.
>
> Chapter kemudian memperluas pertanyaan dari “dividend atau repurchase?” menjadi “**payout atau retain cash?**” Retaining excess cash dapat menimbulkan tax cost dan agency cost, tetapi cash reserves juga dapat mengurangi future financing/issuance costs dan membantu firm menghadapi potential funding shortfalls.
>
> Payout policy juga memiliki **information content**. Managers cenderung melakukan **dividend smoothing**, menaikkan regular dividend hanya jika mereka yakin payout baru sustainable. Karena itu dividend increase/cut dapat menjadi signal mengenai future prospects. Share repurchases juga dapat menjadi signal positif ketika management percaya stock undervalued.
>
> Bagian akhir membedakan **stock dividends, stock splits, reverse splits, dan spin-offs**. Transaksi ini terutama mengubah number of shares atau cara assets dibagi, bukan secara otomatis menciptakan economic value.
>
> Untuk CF4, Chapter 17 sangat relevan dengan **equity instruments, shareholder payouts, share repurchases, capital distribution, dan financing/payout decisions**. Beberapa angka tax dan institutional rules merupakan `[TEXTBOOK CONTEXT]` dari periode edisi buku.

## 1. Why This Chapter Exists

Perusahaan yang menghasilkan cash dari operasi menghadapi tiga broad choices:

```text
Free cash flow
     ↓
┌───────────────┬─────────────────┐
│ Retain cash   │ Pay shareholders│
│ in the firm   │                 │
└───────────────┴─────────────────┘
                         ↓
               ┌─────────┴─────────┐
               │                   │
           Dividends         Share repurchases
```

Ini disebut **payout policy**: keputusan perusahaan mengenai berapa banyak cash yang dibagikan kepada shareholders, kapan dibagikan, dan melalui metode apa.

Pada pandangan pertama, dividend tampak sangat berbeda dari repurchase:

- dividend memberikan cash kepada seluruh eligible shareholders;
- repurchase hanya membayar shareholders yang menjual shares;
- dividend mengurangi share price ketika stock menjadi ex-dividend;
- repurchase mengurangi jumlah shares outstanding.

Tetapi corporate finance tidak berhenti pada accounting appearance. Pertanyaan yang lebih dalam adalah:

> **Apakah shareholders menjadi lebih kaya hanya karena payout dibuat dalam bentuk tertentu?**

Chapter 17 menggunakan kerangka MM untuk memisahkan:

1. **mechanical effect** payout;
2. **value effect** payout;
3. **market imperfections** yang membuat payout method benar-benar matter.

Urutan logic textbook:

```text

      ↓

      ↓
Taxes
      ↓
Investor clienteles
      ↓
Pay out vs retain excess cash
      ↓
Agency and financing costs
      ↓
Information / signaling
      ↓
Stock splits, dividends, spin-offs
```

## 3. 17.1 Distributions to Shareholders

### 3.1 Payout Policy

**Payout policy** adalah policy perusahaan mengenai distribution of cash to shareholders.

Dua primary forms:

1. **cash dividends**;
2. **share repurchases**.

Keduanya mengeluarkan cash dari firm dan mengurangi market value total assets perusahaan. Yang berbeda adalah bagaimana claim shareholders berubah.

### 3.2 Cash Dividends

A **cash dividend** adalah payment cash perusahaan kepada shareholders.

Perusahaan biasanya memiliki **regular dividend policy**, misalnya quarterly dividend, tetapi dapat juga membayar **special dividend** ketika ingin mendistribusikan unusually large amount of cash yang tidak dimaksudkan sebagai recurring payout.

Textbook juga membedakan **liquidating dividend / return of capital**, yaitu distribution yang secara economic/accounting dipandang sebagai pengembalian sebagian invested capital, bukan ordinary distribution dari current/accumulated earnings.

> [!WARNING] Important Distinction
> **Regular dividend ≠ special dividend.**
>
> Regular dividend membawa stronger expectation of continuation. Special dividend lebih mudah dipahami sebagai one-time distribution dan karena itu tidak menciptakan commitment yang sama.

### 3.3 The Dividend Timeline

Dividend memiliki beberapa dates yang harus dibedakan.

#### Declaration Date

**Declaration date** = tanggal board secara resmi mengumumkan dividend.

Pada titik ini management menetapkan dividend amount, record date, dan payable/distribution date.

#### Record Date

**Record date** = tanggal perusahaan menentukan shareholders yang secara resmi tercatat berhak menerima dividend.

#### Ex-Dividend Date

**Ex-dividend date** adalah tanggal stock mulai diperdagangkan **tanpa hak atas dividend yang akan datang**.

Investor yang membeli stock **pada atau setelah ex-dividend date** tidak memperoleh dividend tersebut.

Sebelum ex-dividend date, stock disebut **cum-dividend** karena masih membawa claim atas upcoming dividend.

#### Payable / Distribution Date

**Payable date** atau **distribution date** = tanggal cash dividend benar-benar dibayarkan.

```text
Declaration
     ↓
Stock trades cum-dividend
     ↓
Ex-dividend date
     ↓
Record date
     ↓
Payable / distribution date
```

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Logic
> Yang menentukan price adjustment adalah **hilangnya claim terhadap upcoming dividend**, sehingga economically critical date adalah ex-dividend date, bukan payable date.

### 3.4 Share Repurchases

Dalam **share repurchase**, perusahaan menggunakan cash untuk membeli kembali shares-nya sendiri.

Economic effect:

```text
Firm cash ↓
Shares outstanding ↓
```

Berbeda dengan dividend, tidak semua shareholders harus menerima cash. Cash diterima oleh shareholders yang menjual shares dalam repurchase.

### 3.5 Open-Market Repurchase

Dalam **open-market repurchase**, perusahaan membeli shares di secondary market seperti investor lain.

Characteristics:

- transaksi berlangsung melalui market;
- price sekitar prevailing market price;
- repurchase dapat berlangsung secara bertahap;
- management biasanya mengumumkan authorization/program terlebih dahulu.

Ini menjadi common payout mechanism karena flexible: perusahaan tidak harus melakukan seluruh authorized repurchase sekaligus.

> [!TEXTBOOK CONTEXT]
> Textbook membahas U.S. SEC safe-harbor guidelines untuk open-market repurchases. Detail regulation tersebut bersifat period-specific; lesson utamanya adalah bahwa repurchase dilakukan dalam regulatory framework untuk mengurangi concern mengenai price manipulation.

### 3.6 Tender Offer

Dalam **tender offer**, perusahaan menawarkan untuk membeli shares dari shareholders pada **specified price** selama limited period.

Biasanya firm ingin repurchase relatively large quantity dan offer price berada di atas current market price. Transaction dapat conditional pada jumlah shares yang ditender.

### 3.7 Dutch Auction Repurchase

Dalam **Dutch auction share repurchase**:

1. perusahaan mengumumkan range/list prices yang bersedia dibayar;
2. shareholders menyatakan berapa shares yang bersedia mereka jual pada masing-masing price;
3. perusahaan menentukan **lowest price** yang memungkinkan target jumlah shares dibeli;
4. eligible tendering shareholders menerima clearing price tersebut.

Intuisinya: firm menggunakan supply schedule dari shareholders untuk menentukan minimum price yang cukup untuk memperoleh target shares.

### 3.8 Targeted Repurchase and Greenmail

Dalam **targeted repurchase**, perusahaan membeli shares secara langsung dari specific major shareholder. Price dinegosiasikan bilateral.

Situasinya dapat berbeda:

- shareholder ingin menjual block besar tetapi market tidak cukup liquid → mungkin menerima discount;
- shareholder menjadi takeover threat → management dapat menawarkan premium agar shareholder keluar.

Jika firm membeli shares dari hostile shareholder pada large premium terutama untuk menghentikan takeover threat, transaksi tersebut disebut **greenmail**.

> [!WARNING] Important Distinction
> **Targeted repurchase** adalah kategori direct negotiated repurchase.
>
> **Greenmail** adalah targeted repurchase khusus yang digunakan untuk membeli keluar takeover threat, biasanya dengan premium.

## 4. 17.2 Comparison of Dividends and Share Repurchases

### 4.1 Genron Corporation

Textbook menggunakan **Genron Corporation**.

Assumptions:

- excess cash = $20 million;
- no debt;
- future annual free cash flow = $48 million perpetually;
- unlevered cost of capital = 12%;
- 10 million shares outstanding.

Value operating assets:

$$
EV=\frac{48}{0.12}=400\text{ million}
$$

Including excess cash:

$$
V=400+20=420\text{ million}
$$

Initial share price:

$$
P_{cum}=\frac{420}{10}=\$42
$$

Board mempertimbangkan dua alternatives: $20 million dividend atau $20 million share repurchase.

### 4.2 Alternative 1 — Cash Dividend

Total dividend $20 million. Dengan 10 million shares:

$$
Div=\frac{20}{10}=\$2
$$

Sesudah $20 million cash keluar, firm value menjadi:

$$
420-20=400\text{ million}
$$

Shares tetap 10 million, sehingga ex-dividend share price:

$$
P_{ex}=\frac{400}{10}=\$40
$$

Jadi:

$$
P_{cum}-P_{ex}=42-40=2=Div
$$

> [!NOTE] Perfect-Market Dividend Price Effect
> Dalam perfect capital market:
>
> $$
> \boxed{P_{ex}=P_{cum}-Div}
> $$
>
> atau:
>
> $$
> \boxed{P_{cum}-P_{ex}=Div}
> $$

Dividend tidak menciptakan $2 value baru. Shareholder menerima $2 cash tetapi stock yang dimiliki turun $2.

### 4.3 Alternative 2 — Open-Market Share Repurchase

Jika $20 million digunakan membeli shares pada $42:

$$
\text{Shares repurchased}=\frac{20}{42}=0.47619\text{ million}
$$

Remaining shares:

$$
10-0.47619=9.52381\text{ million}
$$

Setelah cash payout, firm value turun dari $420m menjadi $400m.

Share price:

$$
P_{rep}=\frac{400}{9.52381}\approx\$42
$$

Jadi open-market repurchase **tidak mengubah share price** dalam perfect capital markets.

Karena dua quantities turun proportionally:

```text
Cash assets ↓
        and
shares outstanding ↓
        ↓
Value per remaining share unchanged
```

Future FCF per share juga naik:

$$
\frac{48}{9.52381}=\$5.04
$$

Karena no-growth perpetuity:

$$
P_{rep}=\frac{5.04}{0.12}=\$42
$$

Repurchase menghilangkan current dividend tetapi meningkatkan future cash flow per remaining share.

### 4.4 Investor Wealth: Dividend vs Repurchase

Suppose investor owns 2,000 shares.

Dividend case:

$$
Stock\ value=2000(40)=80{,}000
$$

$$
Cash=2000(2)=4{,}000
$$

Total:

$$
84{,}000
$$

Repurchase case jika investor tidak menjual:

$$
2000(42)=84{,}000
$$

Total wealth sama. Perbedaannya hanya composition.

Investor dapat **homemade payout**:

- jika firm repurchases tetapi investor ingin cash → investor dapat sell shares;
- jika firm pays dividend tetapi investor tidak ingin cash → investor dapat reinvest dividend.

### 4.5 MM Dividend Irrelevance Proposition

> [!NOTE] Modigliani–Miller Dividend Irrelevance
> Dalam **perfect capital markets**, dengan **investment policy fixed**, pilihan perusahaan antara dividend dan share repurchase **tidak memengaruhi initial share price atau shareholder wealth**.

Critical qualifier: **investment policy harus fixed**.

> [!WARNING] Important Distinction
> **Dividend irrelevance ≠ dividends never matter.**
>
> Artinya dividend policy tidak matter **under MM assumptions**. Taxes, transaction costs, agency, dan information dapat membuat payout policy relevant.

## 5. 17.3 The Tax Disadvantage of Dividends

### 5.1 Why Dividends May Be Tax-Disadvantaged

Jika:

$$
t_d>t_g
$$

dengan $t_d$ = dividend tax rate dan $t_g$ = capital gains tax rate, maka investor lebih memilih menerima return sebagai capital gain daripada dividend.

Selain perbedaan rate, capital gains juga dapat memperoleh benefit dari **tax deferral** sampai stock dijual.

### 5.2 Example — Issuing Equity to Pay a Dividend

Firm raises $10 million dari shareholders lalu langsung membayarkan $10 million sebagai dividend.

Tax assumptions:

$$
t_d=40\%,\qquad t_g=15\%
$$

Dividend tax:

$$
10(40\%)=4
$$

million.

Karena firm value turun $10 million saat dividend dibayar, shareholders memiliki $10 million lower capital gain, sehingga capital-gains tax berkurang:

$$
10(15\%)=1.5
$$

million.

Net tax cost:

$$
4-1.5=2.5
$$

million.

Shareholders hanya memperoleh net:

$$
10-2.5=7.5
$$

million dari $10 million yang baru saja mereka investasikan.

Meaning: issuing equity hanya untuk membayar taxable dividend destroys shareholder value through taxes.

### 5.3 Optimal Dividend Policy with Taxes

Jika taxes adalah **satu-satunya** market imperfection dan:

$$
t_d>t_g
$$

maka textbook conclusion: optimal payout policy adalah **no dividends**, dan seluruh payouts dilakukan melalui share repurchases.

> [!TEXTBOOK CONTEXT]
> Tax rules dan rates dalam chapter berasal dari U.S. tax environment pada periode textbook. Durable concept-nya adalah **relative tax treatment of dividends vs capital gains**.

### 5.4 The Dividend Puzzle

Walaupun dividends sering memiliki tax disadvantage, firms tetap membayar dividends dalam jumlah besar. Ini disebut **dividend puzzle**.

Jawabannya dibangun pada sections berikut: tax clienteles, transaction costs, agency considerations, smoothing, dan signaling.

### Figure 17.4 / 17.5 — Historical Shift in Payout Method

Textbook figures menunjukkan historical U.S. pattern:

- proporsi firms paying dividends turun tajam sampai awal 2000-an;
- share repurchases menjadi semakin penting sejak 1980-an;
- pada akhir 1990-an, repurchases melampaui dividends sebagai share of total payouts untuk U.S. industrial firms;
- repurchases turun lebih tajam ketika economic downturns;
- setelah 2003 dividend tax reduction, trend away from dividends sebagian berbalik.

> [!NOTE] Figure Lesson
> **Regular dividends relatif sticky; repurchases jauh lebih flexible dan cyclical.**

## 6. 17.4 Dividend Capture and Tax Clienteles

### 6.1 After-Tax Ex-Dividend Condition

Dividend after tax:

$$
Div(1-t_d)
$$

Capital loss from price drop after tax:

$$
(P_{cum}-P_{ex})(1-t_g)
$$

No-arbitrage after taxes requires:

$$
\boxed{(P_{cum}-P_{ex})(1-t_g)=Div(1-t_d)}
\tag{17.1}
$$

Therefore:

$$
P_{cum}-P_{ex}=Div\left(\frac{1-t_d}{1-t_g}\right)
\tag{17.2}
$$

Textbook defines **effective dividend tax rate**:

$$
\boxed{t_d^*=\frac{t_d-t_g}{1-t_g}}
\tag{17.3}
$$

so:

$$
\boxed{P_{cum}-P_{ex}=Div(1-t_d^*)}
$$

### Meaning of $t_d^*$

$t_d^*$ measures additional tax cost of receiving return as dividend rather than as after-tax capital gain.

If $t_d=t_g$, then $t_d^*=0$ and price should fall by full dividend amount.

If $t_d>t_g$, then $t_d^*>0$.

### 6.2 Example — Effective Dividend Tax Rate

Textbook example for long-term highest-bracket U.S. investor:

2002:

$$
t_d=39\%,\qquad t_g=20\%
$$

$$
t_d^*=\frac{0.39-0.20}{1-0.20}=23.75\%
$$

2003 textbook context:

$$
t_d=t_g=15\%
$$

$$
t_d^*=0
$$

Again, ini historical textbook context.

### 6.3 Why Tax Rates Differ Across Investors

Textbook highlights four reasons:

- **Income Level** — different tax brackets;
- **Investment Horizon** — holding period dan tax deferral;
- **Tax Jurisdiction** — state/country/withholding differences;
- **Investor / Account Type** — taxable individual, retirement account, pension fund, nonprofit, corporation.

### 6.4 Clientele Effects

**Clientele effects** arise when firms attract shareholders whose tax preferences match the firm's dividend policy.

| Investor Type / Situation | Likely Payout Preference in Textbook Logic |
|---|---|
| High-tax taxable investor with $t_d>t_g$ | Lower dividends / repurchases |
| Tax-exempt investor | More neutral |
| Investor needing current income and facing trading costs | Dividends may be convenient |
| Corporation receiving tax-favored dividends in textbook regime | Higher-dividend stocks may be attractive |

### 6.5 Dynamic Clientele / Dividend-Capture Theory

**Dividend-capture theory** proposes that investors can trade around ex-dividend date so low-tax/non-taxed investors receive the dividend.

```text
Before ex-dividend
High-tax investor sells
Low-tax investor buys
        ↓
Dividend captured by lower-tax investor
        ↓
After ex-dividend
Trades can reverse
```

Prediction: unusually high trading volume around ex-dividend dates, especially for large dividends.

### Figure 17.6 — Value Line Special Dividend

Textbook's Figure 17.6 shows Value Line around a **$17.50 special dividend**. Announcement produced a jump in trading volume and stock price; around ex-dividend date, price fell sharply; volume was dramatically higher than normal.

Textbook interprets the pattern as consistent with dividend capture. Transaction costs and stock-price risk make the strategy less attractive for small ordinary dividends.

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Lesson
> Clientele effects dan dividend capture dapat **reduce** dividend tax disadvantage, tetapi tidak necessarily eliminate it.

## 7. 17.5 Payout Versus Retention of Cash

### 7.1 MM Payout Policy Irrelevance

Dalam perfect capital markets, jika excess cash yang retained hanya diinvestasikan pada financial securities, firm value tidak dipengaruhi oleh choice between paying cash now versus retaining and investing cash lalu paying later.

### 7.2 Tax Cost of Retaining Cash

Textbook defines effective tax disadvantage of retaining cash:

$$
\boxed{t_{retain}^*=1-\frac{(1-t_c)(1-t_g)}{1-t_i}}
\tag{17.7}
$$

where:

- $t_c$ = corporate tax rate;
- $t_g$ = investor capital gains tax rate;
- $t_i$ = investor tax rate on interest income.

Relationship:

$$
P_{retain}=P_{cum}(1-t_{retain}^*)
$$

Intuition:

```text
Cash retained in corporation
        ↓
Interest earned
        ↓
Corporate tax
        ↓
Remaining value increases stock value
        ↓
Investor capital-gains tax
```

### 7.3 Textbook Example — Microsoft Excess Cash

Textbook illustrates tax cost of retaining massive cash balances. For additional $32 billion retained, with corporate tax rate 35%, PV of corporate taxes on associated interest income is:

$$
32\text{ billion}(35\%)=11.2\text{ billion}
$$

Equivalent per-share tax saving in the example:

$$
\$3(35\%)=\$1.05
$$

if excess cash is paid out rather than indefinitely retained under those assumptions.

> [!TEXTBOOK CONTEXT]
> This illustrates the mechanism, not a current recommendation about Microsoft or current law.

### 7.4 Why Firms Still Hold Cash — Issuance and Distress Costs

External finance is not free. Future firm may face cash shortfall, large R&D project, acquisition opportunity, volatile earnings, atau difficult access to capital markets.

Cash today can prevent costly financing tomorrow.

Trade-off:

```text
Hold more cash
   ↓
+ financial flexibility
+ avoid future issuance costs
+ fund future positive-NPV projects
- tax cost
- agency cost
```

### 7.5 Agency Costs of Excess Cash

Managers with large discretionary cash can undertake inefficient investments, pursue empire building, consume perks, avoid external capital-market scrutiny, atau lower leverage to protect job security.

This is an **agency cost of free cash flow**.

From shareholder perspective, returning excess cash can limit resources available for value-destroying managerial spending.

### 7.6 Dividend Smoothing

Firms tend not to make regular dividends fluctuate one-for-one with short-term earnings. This is **dividend smoothing**.

Managers typically prefer gradual increases, stable regular payout, and avoiding dividend cuts.

> [!WARNING] Important Distinction
> **Dividend smoothing ≠ constant payout ratio.**
>
> Smoothing means dividend per share is relatively stable, so payout ratio itself may fluctuate as earnings move.

## 8. 17.6 Signaling with Payout Policy

### 8.1 Dividend Signaling Hypothesis

> [!NOTE] Dividend Signaling Hypothesis
> Changes in dividends can reveal management's information or beliefs regarding the firm's future earnings/cash-flow prospects.

Because managers dislike cutting regular dividends, they usually raise dividends only when they believe the higher payout is sustainable.

```text
Dividend increase
      ↓
Management willing to commit to higher recurring payout
      ↓
May signal confidence in future cash flows
```

### 8.2 Why Dividend Cuts Can Be Bad News

If managers expect a temporary earnings decline to reverse, they may maintain dividend using cash reserves or temporary financing. If they finally cut dividend, market may infer management no longer expects near-term recovery sufficient to maintain prior payout.

### 8.3 Share Repurchases as Signals

Repurchases are more flexible than regular dividends, but can signal management's belief that shares are undervalued.

If stock truly undervalued, buying it back benefits remaining long-term shareholders because company purchases shares below intrinsic value. If stock overvalued, repurchase transfers value away from remaining shareholders toward selling shareholders.

Thus managers acting for long-term shareholders should prefer repurchasing when they believe stock is **underpriced**.

> [!TEXTBOOK CONTEXT]
> Textbook reports positive average announcement reactions to repurchase programs, stronger for large tender/Dutch-auction repurchases. Specific percentages are empirical historical estimates.

### 8.4 Example — Repurchase and Market Timing

Clark Industries:

- 200 million shares;
- market price = $30;
- no debt;
- management believes true value = $35;
- plans $600 million repurchase at current market price.

Initial market capitalization:

$$
200(30)=6.0\text{ billion}
$$

Of this, $600m is cash, leaving noncash assets market-valued at:

$$
6.0-0.6=5.4\text{ billion}
$$

Shares repurchased at $30:

$$
\frac{600}{30}=20\text{ million}
$$

Remaining shares:

$$
200-20=180\text{ million}
$$

Management believes pre-payout total intrinsic value is:

$$
200(35)=7.0\text{ billion}
$$

After paying $600m cash:

$$
7.0-0.6=6.4\text{ billion}
$$

Once market learns management's information, value per remaining share becomes:

$$
\frac{6.4\text{ billion}}{180\text{ million}}=\$35.56
$$

Lesson: buying undervalued shares can redistribute value from selling shareholders to continuing shareholders. This differs from the perfect-information MM result because management has **superior information**.

## 9. 17.7 Stock Dividends, Splits, and Spin-Offs

### 9.1 Stock Dividend / Stock Split

A **stock dividend** gives existing shareholders additional shares instead of cash.

A **stock split** similarly increases shares outstanding while reducing value per share proportionally.

For an $n$-for-1 split:

$$
\text{New shares}=n\times\text{Old shares}
$$

and approximately:

$$
\text{New price}=\frac{\text{Old price}}{n}
$$

Example — 2-for-1 split:

Before: 100 shares × $80 = $8,000.

After: 200 shares × about $40 = $8,000.

> [!WARNING] Important Distinction
> **More shares ≠ more wealth.** A stock split changes units, not the economic pie.

### 9.2 Why Firms Split Their Stock

Textbook discusses keeping stock price in a convenient trading range. Potential motives include affordability/accessibility, liquidity, dan transaction-cost considerations.

Figure 17.8 shows most NYSE firms in the textbook period keeping share prices within a relatively moderate range.

### 9.3 Reverse Split

A **reverse split** reduces number of shares outstanding and raises price per share proportionally.

Example:

$$
1:10\text{ reverse split}
$$

means every 10 old shares become 1 new share. If old price is $4.50, new price is approximately $45 absent new information.

Textbook uses Citigroup's 2011 reverse split as historical example.

### 9.4 Spin-Off

A **spin-off** occurs when parent distributes shares of a subsidiary to its shareholders.

After spin-off, shareholders hold parent shares and subsidiary shares, which can trade separately.

It is conceptually different from stock split:

- split divides claim on **same firm** into more units;
- spin-off distributes claim on **different/separated business assets**.

### Textbook Example — Pharmacia / Monsanto

Pharmacia distributed Monsanto shares as special noncash dividend. Each Pharmacia shareholder received a specified fraction of Monsanto shares per Pharmacia share.

At distribution-date Monsanto price, textbook computes value of special dividend of approximately:

$$
\$2.77
$$

per Pharmacia share.

Textbook notes advantages relative to selling Monsanto shares and paying cash: lower transaction costs of the sale and possible tax deferral under the historical tax treatment described.

> [!TEXTBOOK CONTEXT]
> Tax treatment is period- and jurisdiction-specific. Retain the structural distinction: **spin-off separates ownership of a subsidiary rather than merely changing share count.**

## Chapter Synthesis

Chapter 17 can be read as one continuous question:

> **What should a corporation do with cash that is not immediately needed for operations and investment?**

Start with the clean MM benchmark. If capital markets are perfect, shareholders do not care whether cash is delivered through dividends or repurchases. A dividend reduces firm cash and therefore share price by the dividend amount. A repurchase reduces firm cash and shares outstanding at the same time, leaving price per remaining share unchanged. Investors can use trading to manufacture whichever cash pattern they prefer.

Therefore:

$$
\text{Payout packaging alone does not create value.}
$$

Once market imperfections enter, payout policy matters.

**Taxes** may favor repurchases because dividends can be taxed more heavily or sooner than capital gains. But investors differ, producing **tax clienteles**. Investors can also trade around ex-dividend dates, creating **dividend capture**.

The next decision is whether to pay cash at all. **Retaining excess cash** may be tax inefficient and may increase agency problems because managers gain resources they can spend without external discipline. Yet cash also provides financial flexibility and can avoid costly future external financing. Thus optimal cash retention reflects a trade-off.

Regular dividends are difficult to cut without disappointing investors. Managers therefore practice **dividend smoothing**, making regular dividend changes relatively rare and persistent. This turns dividends into potential **signals** about long-run expected cash flows. Repurchases can also signal undervaluation because buying underpriced stock benefits continuing shareholders.

Finally, stock splits and stock dividends remind us not to confuse changes in **units** with changes in **value**. A split increases shares and proportionally reduces price; a reverse split does the opposite. A spin-off is different: it separates an asset/subsidiary into independently tradable ownership claims.

The overarching lesson mirrors Chapter 14:

```text
Financial packaging by itself
        ↓
does not create economic value
        ↓
Value effects arise when payout changes
Taxes / transaction costs / financing costs /
agency incentives / information / investment
```

## Quick Reading Review

- **Payout policy** menentukan apakah free cash flow didistribusikan atau ditahan.
- Primary cash payout methods adalah **dividends** dan **share repurchases**.
- Buyer pada/after **ex-dividend date** tidak menerima upcoming dividend.
- Dalam perfect market:
  $$
  P_{ex}=P_{cum}-Div
  $$
- Open-market repurchase mengurangi cash dan share count secara proporsional, sehingga share price unchanged under perfect-market assumptions.
- **MM dividend irrelevance:** dengan investment policy fixed dan perfect capital markets, payout method tidak memengaruhi initial share price.
- Jika $t_d>t_g$, dividends memiliki tax disadvantage relative to capital gains.
- Effective dividend tax rate:
  $$
  t_d^*=\frac{t_d-t_g}{1-t_g}
  $$
- Different investors face different taxes → **clientele effects**.
- **Dividend capture** memindahkan dividend ke lower-tax investors melalui trading around ex-dividend dates.
- Retaining excess cash dapat tax-costly tetapi juga menghindari future financing costs.
- Too much discretionary cash dapat menciptakan **agency costs**.
- Firms generally practice **dividend smoothing**.
- Dividend increases dapat signal confidence; cuts dapat signal weaker prospects.
- Repurchases dapat signal management believes stock is undervalued.
- **Tender offer:** specified repurchase price selama limited period.
- **Dutch auction:** shareholders reveal willingness to sell across prices; firm determines clearing price.
- **Targeted repurchase:** direct negotiated purchase from major holder.
- **Greenmail:** targeted premium repurchase to remove takeover threat.
- Stock split increases shares dan proportionally lowers price; total value unchanged absent new information.
- Reverse split reduces shares dan raises nominal price.
- Spin-off distributes ownership of a subsidiary, creating separately tradable claims.
