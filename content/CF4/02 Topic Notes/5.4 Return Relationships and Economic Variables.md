# 📘 5.4 — Return Relationships and Economic Variables

> [!ABSTRACT] Ringkasan Cepat
> **Topik:** Return Relationships and Economic Variables  
> **Bobot Parent Topic:** 10–20%  
> **Exam Priority:** Medium  
> **Difficulty:** Concept-Intensive  
> **Primary Skill:** Menjelaskan hubungan **teoritis** dan **historis** antara total return, risk, dan economic variables pada kelompok aset utama.
>
> Inti topik ini adalah membedakan tiga hal yang sering tercampur: **historical realized return**, **expected return**, dan **required return**. Berk & DeMarzo menunjukkan bahwa secara historis, untuk broad U.S. asset portfolios selama 1926–2011, kelompok aset dengan return lebih volatile umumnya memberikan average return dan excess return yang lebih tinggi: small stocks berada di atas large stocks, lalu corporate bonds, lalu Treasury bills. Tetapi historical average bukan jaminan future return dan bahkan estimasi expected return dari puluhan tahun data tetap sangat tidak presisi.
>
> Teori kemudian memperbaiki narasi sederhana “higher volatility → higher return”. Investor dapat menghilangkan banyak **firm-specific risk** melalui diversification. Karena itu risk premium tidak ditentukan oleh seluruh stand-alone volatility, tetapi oleh **systematic risk**—sensitivity terhadap shocks yang memengaruhi market/economy. Beta mengukur sensitivity tersebut, dan CAPM menghubungkannya dengan required expected return:
>
> $$
> r_i=r_f+\beta_i\left(E[R_M]-r_f\right)
> $$
>
> Economic variables seperti interest rates, inflation, business conditions, dan market-wide shocks penting karena dapat menjadi sumber systematic movement dalam returns. Namun historical correlation tidak boleh langsung dianggap sebagai causal law atau future guarantee. Market prices sudah mengandung expectations; karena itu **surprise/new information**, bukan sekadar level variabel ekonomi, sering menjadi pemicu repricing.
>
> Untuk ujian: pahami **risk-return hierarchy, arithmetic vs compound return, excess return, volatility, diversification, systematic risk, beta, CAPM, expected vs realized return, dan keterbatasan historical evidence**.

# Section 1 — Big Picture: Dari Economic Variables ke Return

Untuk memahami 5.4, gunakan dua lapisan.

### Lapisan A — Asset Return

Investor memegang asset selama suatu periode dan menerima:

```text
Income
+
change in market value
=
total return
```

Untuk saham:

$$
R_t
=
\frac{Div_t+P_t-P_{t-1}}{P_{t-1}}
$$

Untuk bond secara konseptual:

$$
\text{Total Return}
=
\text{Coupon/Interest Income}
+
\text{Capital Gain/Loss}
$$

dibandingkan dengan initial price.

### Lapisan B — Economic Drivers

Economic variables dapat mengubah:

1. expected cash flows;
2. required return / discount rate;
3. risk premium;
4. market price.

Sehingga:

```text
Economic variable changes
        ↓
cash-flow expectations / discount rates / risk premiums
        ↓
asset repricing
        ↓
capital gain or loss
        ↓
realized total return
```

> [!WARNING] Important Distinction
> **Economic variable dan asset return bukan identik.**
>
> Return adalah outcome pada security. Economic variable adalah salah satu possible driver dari cash flows, discount rates, atau market-wide risk.

# Section 2 — Total Return dan Komponennya

## 2.1 Stock Total Return

Berk & DeMarzo mendefinisikan realized/total return dari \(t-1\) ke \(t\):

$$
R_t
=
\frac{Div_t+P_t-P_{t-1}}{P_{t-1}}
$$

Pisahkan menjadi:

$$
R_t
=
\frac{Div_t}{P_{t-1}}
+
\frac{P_t-P_{t-1}}{P_{t-1}}
$$

atau:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Total Return}
=
\text{Dividend Yield}
+
\text{Capital Gain Yield}
}
$$

Contoh sederhana:

- \(P_0=100\)
- dividend \(=4\)
- \(P_1=110\)

maka:

$$
R
=
\frac{4+110-100}{100}
=
14\%
$$

terdiri dari:

$$
Dividend\ Yield=4\%
$$

dan:

$$
Capital\ Gain\ Yield=10\%
$$

### Financial Meaning

Economic conditions dapat memengaruhi dua components tersebut secara berbeda.

- weaker profits dapat menekan dividend expectation;
- higher required return dapat menurunkan \(P_1\);
- higher growth expectation dapat menaikkan \(P_1\);
- realized return merupakan gabungan actual cash distribution dan repricing.

---

## 2.2 Expected Stock Return dalam Constant-Growth Framework

Brigham Chapter 7 memberi relationship:

$$
\hat r_s
=
\frac{D_1}{P_0}+g
$$

Dengan constant growth:

- \(\frac{D_1}{P_0}\) = expected dividend yield;
- \(g\) = expected growth rate, yang juga menjadi expected capital-gains yield dalam model.

Maka:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Expected Stock Return}
=
\text{Expected Dividend Yield}
+
\text{Expected Capital-Gains Yield}
}
$$

> [!BUG] Exam Trap
> **Historical capital gain** tidak sama dengan **expected growth rate**.  
> Formula constant-growth adalah forward-looking valuation relationship dengan assumptions tertentu.

# Section 3 — Expected, Required, dan Realized Return

Ini adalah distinction paling penting di Topik 5.4.

| Return Concept | Meaning | Time Perspective |
|---|---|---|
| **Expected return** | Probability-weighted return yang diperkirakan investor | Ex ante |
| **Required return** | Return minimum yang diminta sebagai compensation untuk time value + relevant risk | Ex ante |
| **Realized return** | Return yang benar-benar terjadi | Ex post |
| **Historical average return** | Average dari realized returns masa lalu | Ex post data |

Dalam competitive equilibrium:

$$
\text{Expected Return}
\approx
\text{Required Return}
$$

tetapi realized return dapat jauh berbeda karena actual outcomes dan new information.

### Mental Model

```text
Today:
investor sets expectations
        ↓
required return reflects risk
        ↓
market price forms

Later:
actual news/outcomes arrive
        ↓
price and cash flow differ from expectation
        ↓
realized return
```

> [!DANGER] Exam Trap
> **High required return does not mean high realized return is guaranteed.**
>
> Required return adalah price of risk **before outcome is known**.

Higher volatility

# Section 5 — Historical Risk-Return Trade-Off

## 5.1 Apa yang Data Tunjukkan?

Berk & DeMarzo Figure 10.6 menunjukkan positive relationship secara umum antara:

```text
historical volatility
and
historical average return
```

untuk broad portfolios.

Ini konsisten dengan intuition risk aversion:

> investor tidak mau menanggung more risk tanpa expectation memperoleh compensation.

Namun hubungan ini belum cukup menjadi final pricing theory.

Mengapa?

Karena **volatility total** dapat mengandung risk yang bisa dihilangkan melalui diversification.

---

## 5.2 Broad Portfolio vs Individual Security

Untuk large diversified portfolios, much firm-specific noise sudah saling offset. Maka historical volatility pada portfolio lebih dekat mencerminkan common risks.

Untuk individual stock:

```text
Total volatility
=
systematic component
+
firm-specific component
```

Firm-specific component tidak harus menghasilkan risk premium karena diversified investor tidak perlu menanggungnya.

> [!WARNING] Important Distinction
> Data antar **broad asset portfolios** dapat menunjukkan “higher volatility ↔ higher average return”, tetapi untuk **individual securities**, stand-alone volatility bukan sufficient measure dari priced risk.

---

# Section 6 — Arithmetic Average vs Compound Annual Return

Ini merupakan exam-friendly distinction.

## 6.1 Arithmetic Average Return

Untuk \(T\) annual returns:

$$
\bar R
=
\frac{1}{T}
\sum_{t=1}^{T}R_t
$$

Arithmetic average digunakan oleh textbook sebagai estimate expected annual return jika historical observations diperlakukan sebagai draws dari distribution yang sama.

---

## 6.2 Compound Annual Return / CAGR

$$
R_{compound}
=
\left[
(1+R_1)(1+R_2)\cdots(1+R_T)
\right]^{1/T}-1
$$

Equivalent form:

$$
R_{compound}
=
\left(
\frac{Final\ Value}{Initial\ Value}
\right)^{1/T}-1
$$

Compound annual return lebih cocok untuk menjelaskan:

> actual long-run growth rate yang menyamakan beginning wealth dengan ending wealth.

---

## 6.3 Volatility Drag

Jika returns:

- Year 1 = \(+20\%\)
- Year 2 = \(-20\%\)

Arithmetic average:

$$
\bar R
=
\frac{20\%-20\%}{2}
=
0\%
$$

Tetapi wealth:

$$
1(1.20)(0.80)
=
0.96
$$

Compound annual return:

$$
R_{compound}
=
0.96^{1/2}-1
\approx
-2.02\%
$$

Textbook lesson:

> compound annual return berada di bawah arithmetic average ketika return berfluktuasi; gap membesar ketika volatility meningkat.

Historical textbook CAGR 1926–2011:

| Investment | Compound Annual Return |
|---|---:|
| Small stocks | 12.6% |
| S&P 500 | 9.65% |
| Corporate bonds | 6.4% |
| Treasury bills | 3.6% |

### One-Line Memory

```text
Arithmetic average → estimate one-period expected return

Compound/CAGR → realized long-run wealth growth
```

> [!BUG] Exam Trap
> Jangan memilih geometric return hanya karena horizon datanya panjang jika pertanyaannya meminta **estimate expected one-period return** dari historical sample. Sebaliknya, jangan menggunakan arithmetic average untuk menyatakan actual compound wealth growth.

# Section 7 — Why Historical Average Is an Imprecise Expected-Return Estimate

Historical data noisy.

Berk & DeMarzo menggunakan standard error:

$$
SE(\bar R)
=
\frac{SD(R)}{\sqrt{T}}
$$

Approximate 95% confidence interval:

$$
\bar R
\pm
2SE(\bar R)
$$

Untuk S&P 500 dalam 1926–2011:

- average return \(=11.7\%\);
- volatility \(=20.3\%\);
- \(T=86\).

Textbook estimate:

$$
SE
=
\frac{20.3\%}{\sqrt{86}}
$$

sehingga approximate 95% interval:

$$
11.7\%
\pm
4.4\%
$$

atau kira-kira:

$$
7.3\%
\text{ to }
16.1\%
$$

### Interpretation

Bahkan **86 tahun data** masih menghasilkan range expected-return estimate yang lebar.

Untuk shorter period, uncertainty jauh lebih besar.

Textbook example 2002–2011:

- average S&P 500 return \(=5.0\%\);
- volatility \(=20.5\%\);
- approximate 95% interval \(=-8\%\) sampai \(18\%\).

> [!TIP] Exam Implication
> Historical average adalah **estimate**, bukan truth. High volatility membuat mean return sulit diestimasi secara precise.

# Section 8 — Diversification: Mengapa Tidak Semua Risk Dibayar

## 8.1 Firm-Specific Risk

Risk yang khusus pada perusahaan dapat berasal dari events seperti:

- product success/failure;
- management event;
- operational disruption;
- firm-specific demand.

Jika investor memegang banyak securities, positive dan negative firm-specific shocks dapat saling offset.

```text
More independent securities
→ firm-specific risk diversified away
```

---

## 8.2 Systematic Risk

Systematic risk berasal dari common shocks yang memengaruhi banyak investments sekaligus.

Examples dalam framing source:

- market-wide economic shocks;
- broad interest-rate changes;
- oil-price changes jika memengaruhi economy/industries luas;
- common macro conditions.

Risk ini tidak hilang hanya dengan menambah banyak stocks.

```text
Diversification
removes firm-specific risk
but
cannot remove market-wide systematic risk
```

### Pricing Implication

Investor hanya harus diberi compensation untuk risk yang tetap mereka tanggung setelah optimal diversification.

Maka:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Priced Risk}
=
\text{Systematic Risk}
}
$$

dalam CAPM framework.

---

# Section 9 — Economic Variables sebagai Systematic Shocks

Berk & DeMarzo secara eksplisit menjelaskan sensitivity analogy:

- sensitivity stock return terhadap interest-rate change;
- sensitivity stock return terhadap oil-price change;
- sensitivity stock return terhadap market-portfolio return.

Ini penting untuk learning outcome 5.4.

## 9.1 General Sensitivity Concept

Jika suatu asset memiliki high exposure terhadap economic variable \(X\):

```text
Economic shock X
→ relatively large asset-return response
```

Tetapi CAPM tidak menggunakan satu specific macro variable sebagai sole pricing factor. Ia menggunakan **market portfolio return** sebagai aggregate representation dari systematic shocks.

---

## 9.2 Interest Rates

Theoretical direction dari [[5.3 Economic Influences on Markets]]:

```text
market yield ↑
→ fixed-rate bond price ↓
```

Untuk equity:

```text
interest rate ↑
→ required return may ↑
→ valuation may ↓
```

tetapi equity cash flows juga dapat berubah bersama economic conditions.

Historical co-movement therefore tidak harus one-to-one atau constant.

---

## 9.3 Inflation

Expected inflation dapat masuk nominal required returns:

```text
inflation expectations ↑
→ nominal required return ↑
```

Real return harus membedakan purchasing-power effect.

Approximation:

$$
r_{real}
\approx
r_{nominal}-\pi
$$

Exact:

$$
1+r_{nominal}
=
(1+r_{real})(1+\pi)
$$

Namun stock cash flows dapat ikut berubah dengan price levels, wages, costs, dan nominal revenues. Maka equity–inflation relationship tidak sesederhana fixed nominal debt.

---

## 9.4 Business Conditions

Firms yang profits-nya sangat cyclical cenderung memiliki returns yang kuat ketika economy kuat dan buruk ketika economy lemah.

Jika return bergerak kuat bersama market/economy:

```text
systematic sensitivity ↑
→ beta ↑
→ required risk premium ↑
```

This is the economic meaning of beta—not merely a statistical number.

---

# Section 10 — Market Portfolio dan Beta

## 10.1 Market Portfolio

Berk & DeMarzo menggunakan **market portfolio** sebagai natural candidate untuk broad efficient portfolio berisi traded securities.

Dalam practice, textbook menyebut S&P 500 sering digunakan sebagai approximation karena data lebih mudah diperoleh.

Market portfolio digunakan sebagai benchmark systematic shocks.

---

## 10.2 Beta

Beta mengukur sensitivity security return terhadap market return.

Secara conceptual:

> **Beta = expected percentage change dalam security return untuk setiap 1% change dalam market-portfolio return.**

Statistical form:

$$
\beta_i
=
\frac{Cov(R_i,R_M)}
{Var(R_M)}
$$

Interpretasi:

- \(\beta=1\): market sensitivity sekitar average market;
- \(\beta>1\): amplified market sensitivity;
- \(0<\beta<1\): same direction but less sensitive;
- \(\beta=0\): no systematic sensitivity in CAPM sense;
- \(\beta<0\): tends to offset market movements; can provide insurance-like benefit.

> [!BUG] Exam Trap
> **Beta bukan probability of loss.**  
> **Beta bukan standard deviation.**  
> **Beta bukan correlation saja.**
>
> Beta mengukur sensitivity terhadap market factor.

---

# Section 11 — CAPM: Theoretical Return Relationship

Berk & DeMarzo Chapter 10 memberi:

$$
\boxed{
r_i
=
r_f
+
\beta_i
\left(
E[R_M]-r_f
\right)
}
$$

Dengan:

- \(r_i\) = required / cost of capital untuk asset \(i\);
- \(r_f\) = risk-free rate;
- \(E[R_M]-r_f\) = market risk premium;
- \(\beta_i\) = systematic risk exposure.

Equivalent:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Required Return}
=
\text{Risk-Free Rate}
+
\text{Beta}
\times
\text{Market Risk Premium}
}
$$

---

## 11.1 Economic Meaning

CAPM memisahkan compensation menjadi dua:

### Time value of money

$$
r_f
$$

### Compensation for systematic risk

$$
\beta_i(E[R_M]-r_f)
$$

Sehingga:

```text
Higher beta
→ higher required risk premium
→ higher required expected return
```

---

## 11.2 Example

Jika:

$$
r_f=5\%
$$

$$
E[R_M]-r_f=6\%
$$

dan:

$$
\beta=1.48
$$

maka:

$$
r
=
5\%+1.48(6\%)
=
13.88\%
\approx
13.9\%
$$

Untuk \(\beta=0.20\):

$$
r
=
5\%+0.20(6\%)
=
6.2\%
$$

Textbook uses this type of comparison to explain why securities with different systematic exposure can have very different expected returns.

---

# Section 12 — Negative Beta

CAPM memungkinkan:

$$
\beta_i<0
$$

Maka:

$$
r_i<r_f
$$

jika market risk premium positif.

Mengapa investor mau menerima expected return di bawah risk-free rate?

Karena asset dengan negative beta dapat:

```text
perform well in bad market states
→ hedge portfolio systematic risk
→ provide insurance value
```

Investor bersedia “membayar” insurance tersebut melalui lower expected return.

> [!DANGER] Exam Trap
> “Risky security must always have expected return above risk-free rate” **salah** dalam CAPM jika security memiliki negative beta.

---

# Section 13 — Market Risk Premium

Market risk premium:

$$
RPM
=
E[R_M]-r_f
$$

Historical excess return adalah backward-looking analog:

$$
Historical\ Premium
=
\bar R_M-\bar R_f
$$

Tetapi keduanya tidak identik.

## 13.1 Why Historical Premium Is Difficult

Brigham menjelaskan beberapa limitations:

1. stock returns volatile → estimate noisy;
2. average sangat sensitive terhadap sample period;
3. investors' risk aversion dapat berubah;
4. perubahan required premium sendiri dapat menyebabkan immediate opposite price movement.

Contoh conceptual:

```text
Investor risk aversion ↑
→ required premium ↑
→ discount rate ↑
→ current stock price ↓
→ observed short-run return can be negative
```

Jadi saat required future return naik, **current realized return justru dapat turun** karena repricing.

> [!WARNING] Critical Distinction
> **Higher required future return can create a negative current price return.**
>
> Ini salah satu alasan historical realized premium tidak boleh dibaca mechanically sebagai current required premium.

---

# Section 14 — Historical Relationships ≠ Future Guarantees

## 14.1 Sampling Uncertainty

Historical data hanya satu realization dari random process.

High volatility membuat mean return sangat noisy.

---

## 14.2 Regime Changes

Distribution returns dapat berubah karena:

- financial structure;
- economic environment;
- investor risk tolerance;
- market institutions.

Source menekankan bahwa jika return distribution berubah over time, historical observations menjadi even less reliable untuk estimating future return.

---

## 14.3 Price Already Reflects Expectations

Chapter 13 emphasizes competitive market reaction to new information.

Jika information sudah expected:

> market price dapat sudah mencerminkan informasi tersebut.

Maka relationship yang penting bukan hanya:

```text
GDP high / inflation high / earnings high
```

tetapi:

```text
actual information
versus
what market previously expected
```

### Example

Suppose earnings grow 10%.

- jika market expected 5% → positive surprise;
- jika market expected 20% → negative surprise.

Sama-sama “earnings naik”, tetapi price reaction dapat opposite.

---

# Section 15 — New Information, Alpha, dan Price Adjustment

Dalam Chapter 13, expected security return dibandingkan dengan required return menurut CAPM.

CAPM required return:

$$
r_i
=
r_f+\beta_i(E[R_M]-r_f)
$$

Alpha secara conceptual:

$$
\alpha_i
=
E[R_i]-r_i
$$

Jika:

$$
\alpha_i>0
$$

security menawarkan expected return di atas required return menurut model.

Competitive investors akan memiliki incentive membeli security tersebut.

Buying pressure:

```text
demand ↑
→ price ↑
→ expected future return ↓
```

sampai mispricing/opportunity berkurang.

---

## 15.1 Why News Moves Prices

Jika good news meningkatkan expected future cash flow dan current price belum berubah:

```text
expected return would rise
→ security becomes attractive
→ investors buy
→ current price rises
→ expected return normalizes relative to risk
```

Competitive trading therefore rapidly embeds new information into prices.

---

# Section 16 — Empirical Departures from Simple CAPM

Chapter 13 menunjukkan bahwa empirical evidence tidak selalu sepenuhnya cocok dengan simple CAPM benchmark.

Examples discussed:

- size effect;
- book-to-market/value effect;
- momentum.

Textbook presents these as evidence yang dapat berarti salah satu dari dua hal:

1. markets contain persistent opportunities because investors do not fully exploit them / trading frictions matter; atau
2. CAPM beta tidak menangkap seluruh systematic risk yang investors care about.

> [!IMPORTANT] CF4 Scope
> Untuk CF4, lesson utamanya bukan menghafal advanced factor-model coefficients.
>
> Lesson-nya:
>
> **Theoretical model gives a benchmark; historical return patterns may show departures, so empirical relationships must be interpreted carefully.**

---

## 16.1 Momentum — Textbook Historical Evidence

Chapter 13 cites historical research where stocks with strong prior 6–12 month performance showed positive alpha over subsequent months, and vice versa.

This challenges simple CAPM/market-efficiency interpretation if taken literally.

Do **not** convert this into:

> “past winners will always outperform.”

The source treats momentum as empirical evidence requiring explanation, not a guaranteed trading rule.

# Section 17 — Return Relationships by Asset Class

## 17.1 Treasury Bills

Historical characteristics in Berk & DeMarzo dataset:

- lowest average return among compared asset classes;
- lowest volatility;
- used as short-term risk-free benchmark in historical excess-return analysis.

Economic sensitivities:

- returns reprice with short-term rates;
- reinvestment rates change relatively quickly;
- low price volatility compared with long-duration debt.

### Core Relationship

```text
Low systematic/market exposure
→ low historical risk premium
```

---

## 17.2 Corporate Bonds

Historical characteristics:

- average return above Treasury bills;
- volatility above Treasury bills but much below equities in textbook sample;
- positive historical excess return.

Economic drivers:

- risk-free interest rates;
- inflation expectations;
- credit spread/default conditions;
- business cycle.

Key theoretical relationship for fixed-rate bonds:

$$
Yield\uparrow
\Rightarrow
Price\downarrow
$$

Hence return can include significant capital gain/loss despite fixed coupon.

---

## 17.3 Large Stocks / Broad Equity Market

Historical characteristics:

- higher average return than bonds/bills;
- much higher volatility;
- substantial drawdowns in bad periods.

Theoretical compensation:

```text
systematic exposure
→ positive risk premium
```

Stock return depends on:

- dividend/cash-flow outcomes;
- changes in expected growth;
- changes in required return;
- market-wide shocks.

---

## 17.4 Small Stocks

Historical textbook sample shows:

- highest average return;
- highest volatility;
- largest historical excess return among listed classes.

But:

> historical small-stock premium does not by itself prove that **size alone** is the causal priced risk.

Chapter 13 notes size-related alpha as possible evidence against simple CAPM efficiency or as evidence that beta omits relevant risk.

# Section 18 — Real vs Nominal Return

Economic interpretation requires distinguishing purchasing power.

Exact Fisher-style relationship:

$$
1+r_n
=
(1+r_r)(1+\pi)
$$

so:

$$
r_r
=
\frac{1+r_n}{1+\pi}-1
$$

Approximation:

$$
r_r
\approx
r_n-\pi
$$

Example:

Nominal return \(=10\%\), inflation \(=6\%\).

Approximate:

$$
r_r
\approx
4\%
$$

Exact:

$$
r_r
=
\frac{1.10}{1.06}-1
\approx
3.77\%
$$

### Interpretation

An asset may generate positive nominal return tetapi weaker real wealth growth.

> [!BUG] Exam Trap
> Jika inflation tinggi, jangan menilai investment outcome hanya dari nominal return.

# Section 19 — Relationship Matrix: Variable → Asset Return

Tabel berikut adalah **theoretical first-pass relationship**, bukan universal empirical law.

| Economic Variable / Shock | Treasury Bills | Fixed-Rate Bonds | Equities | Main Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short-term rates ↑ | Future/reinvestment yield ↑ | Price pressure if market yields ↑ | Higher discount rate can pressure prices | Rate channel |
| Long-term market yields ↑ | Limited direct price effect if maturity short | Price ↓ | Required return can ↑ | Discount rate |
| Expected inflation ↑ | Nominal rates tend ↑ | Fixed nominal CF less valuable | Mixed: discount-rate vs nominal CF effect | Inflation channel |
| Business growth ↑ | Rates may ↑ | Yield effect potentially negative on prices | Earnings/cash flow may ↑; rate effect can offset | Cash flow + rates |
| Recession / broad downturn | Rates may fall; depends on policy | Government-like bonds may benefit from rate fall; corporate credit can worsen | Earnings ↓, risk premium may ↑ | Cash flow + risk |
| Risk aversion ↑ | Demand for safer assets may ↑ | Safe bonds can benefit; risky bonds may fall | Required risk premium ↑ → price ↓ | Risk premium |
| Credit/default risk ↑ | Little direct issuer-credit effect | Corporate bond spread ↑ → price ↓ | Issuer equity usually also pressured | Credit risk |
| Positive market shock | Small sensitivity | Depends on rates/credit | Higher-beta stocks respond more | Systematic beta |

> [!CAUTION]
> Bila dua channels bergerak opposite, direction net tidak dapat ditentukan tanpa additional information.

# Section 20 — Worked Examples

## Example 1 — Arithmetic vs Compound Return

**Situation**

Asset returns:

- Year 1: \(+30\%\)
- Year 2: \(-20\%\)

### Arithmetic Average

$$
\bar R
=
\frac{30\%-20\%}{2}
=
5\%
$$

### Wealth Outcome

$$
1(1.30)(0.80)
=
1.04
$$

### CAGR

$$
R_{compound}
=
1.04^{1/2}-1
\approx
1.98\%
$$

### Meaning

Average annual return \(5\%\) does **not** mean wealth compounded at 5%.

---

## Example 2 — Excess Return

Suppose:

- stock return \(=12\%\);
- T-bill/risk-free return \(=4\%\).

Then:

$$
Excess\ Return
=
12\%-4\%
=
8\%
$$

This is the realized premium for that period.

It is **not automatically** the required future market risk premium.

---

## Example 3 — CAPM

Suppose:

$$
r_f=4\%
$$

$$
E[R_M]=10\%
$$

$$
\beta_i=1.25
$$

Market risk premium:

$$
10\%-4\%
=
6\%
$$

Security required return:

$$
r_i
=
4\%+1.25(6\%)
=
11.5\%
$$

Interpretation:

Security bears 1.25 times market systematic sensitivity, so it receives a risk premium 1.25 times market premium under CAPM.

---

## Example 4 — Negative Beta

Suppose:

$$
r_f=4\%
$$

$$
E[R_M]-r_f=6\%
$$

$$
\beta=-0.5
$$

Then:

$$
r
=
4\%-0.5(6\%)
=
1\%
$$

Expected return below risk-free rate is possible because security provides hedge value against market downturns.

---

## Example 5 — Risk Premium Repricing

Suppose expected cash flows do not change but investor risk aversion rises.

```text
Required market risk premium ↑
→ required return ↑
→ current prices ↓
```

Therefore current realized return may be negative even though future required return has increased.

This is one reason “high current required return” and “high current realized return” are not the same thing.

# Section 22 — Core Exam Traps

> [!BUG] **Trap 1 — “Higher volatility always means higher expected return.”**
>
> Salah untuk individual assets. Diversifiable volatility does not earn a risk premium in CAPM.

> [!BUG] **Trap 2 — “Historical average return is the expected future return.”**
>
> Salah. Historical average is only an estimate and may have large estimation error.

> [!BUG] **Trap 3 — “Arithmetic average equals long-run compound growth.”**
>
> Salah jika returns fluctuate.

> [!BUG] **Trap 4 — “Higher required return means current realized return rises.”**
>
> Salah. Higher required return often causes immediate price decline.

> [!BUG] **Trap 5 — “Beta is total risk.”**
>
> Salah. Beta is systematic market sensitivity.

> [!BUG] **Trap 6 — “Negative beta is impossible because risky assets must beat the risk-free asset.”**
>
> Salah. Negative-beta asset can provide insurance and therefore have expected return below \(r_f\).

> [!BUG] **Trap 7 — “Good economic data always produces positive stock return.”**
>
> Salah. Market reaction depends on what had already been expected and on discount-rate effects.

> [!BUG] **Trap 8 — “Corporate bond coupon is its total return.”**
>
> Salah. Market-price movement creates capital gain/loss.

> [!BUG] **Trap 9 — “Observed correlation proves causal relationship.”**
>
> Salah. Historical co-movement may reflect common variables, changing regimes, or omitted risks.

> [!BUG] **Trap 10 — “Positive alpha proves free money.”**
>
> Tidak otomatis. Chapter 13 presents alternative interpretations: market/friction inefficiency or model misspecification/unmeasured risk.

# Section 23 — Formula Sheet

## Total Stock Return

$$
R_t
=
\frac{Div_t+P_t-P_{t-1}}{P_{t-1}}
$$

## Dividend Yield

$$
Dividend\ Yield
=
\frac{Div_t}{P_{t-1}}
$$

## Capital Gain Yield

$$
Capital\ Gain\ Yield
=
\frac{P_t-P_{t-1}}{P_{t-1}}
$$

## Expected Return

$$
E[R]
=
\sum_i p_iR_i
$$

## Arithmetic Historical Average

$$
\bar R
=
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}R_t
$$

## Compound Annual Return

$$
R_{compound}
=
\left[
\prod_{t=1}^{T}(1+R_t)
\right]^{1/T}-1
$$

## Historical Variance Estimate

$$
Var(R)
=
\frac{1}{T-1}
\sum_{t=1}^{T}(R_t-\bar R)^2
$$

## Historical Volatility

$$
SD(R)
=
\sqrt{Var(R)}
$$

## Standard Error of Historical Mean

$$
SE(\bar R)
=
\frac{SD(R)}{\sqrt{T}}
$$

## Excess Return

$$
R_{excess}
=
R_i-R_f
$$

## Beta

$$
\beta_i
=
\frac{Cov(R_i,R_M)}
{Var(R_M)}
$$

## CAPM

$$
r_i
=
r_f+\beta_i(E[R_M]-r_f)
$$

## Market Risk Premium

$$
RPM
=
E[R_M]-r_f
$$

## Alpha

$$
\alpha_i
=
E[R_i]
-
\left[
r_f+\beta_i(E[R_M]-r_f)
\right]
$$

## Nominal–Real Return Relationship

$$
1+r_n
=
(1+r_r)(1+\pi)
$$

## Approximate Real Return

$$
r_r
\approx
r_n-\pi
$$

| Concept | What It Measures | Best Use | Main Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total return | Income + price change | Holding-period performance | Forget capital gain/loss |
| Arithmetic average | Mean periodic realized return | Estimate expected one-period return under stable distribution assumption | Treat as CAGR |
| CAGR | Compound wealth growth | Long-run historical performance | Treat as expected annual return |
| Standard deviation | Total return dispersion | Stand-alone volatility | Assume all volatility priced |
| Excess return | Return above risk-free benchmark | Historical/expected risk premium concept | Assume historical = required |
| Systematic risk | Non-diversifiable economic/market exposure | Pricing relevant risk | Confuse with total risk |
| Beta | Market-return sensitivity | CAPM required return | Treat as probability |

| Alpha | Return relative to model benchmark | Identify model-relative abnormal expected/performed return | “Positive alpha = guaranteed arbitrage” |
| Real return | Purchasing-power growth | Inflation-adjusted performance | Subtract inflation mechanically when exact requested |

# Section 25 — Active Recall

> [!QUESTION]- 1. Apa dua komponen total return saham?
> **Dividend yield** dan **capital gain/loss yield**.

> [!QUESTION]- 2. Apa perbedaan expected, required, dan realized return?
> Expected = return yang diperkirakan; required = compensation minimum untuk time value + relevant risk; realized = actual ex-post outcome.

> [!QUESTION]- 3. Apa historical ranking average return Berk & DeMarzo 1926–2011?
> **Small stocks > S&P 500 > corporate bonds > Treasury bills.**

> [!QUESTION]- 4. Apakah historical ranking tersebut menjamin future ranking?
> **Tidak.** Historical data adalah sample, noisy, dan distribution dapat berubah.

> [!QUESTION]- 5. Apa perbedaan arithmetic average dan CAGR?
> Arithmetic average = average periodic return; CAGR = constant compound rate yang menghubungkan initial dan final wealth.

> [!QUESTION]- 6. Mengapa CAGR biasanya di bawah arithmetic average ketika returns volatile?
> Karena multiplicative wealth process mengalami volatility drag; losses dan gains dengan percentage sama tidak saling membatalkan pada wealth.

> [!QUESTION]- 7. Risk apa yang dapat largely eliminated melalui diversification?
> **Firm-specific / idiosyncratic risk.**

> [!QUESTION]- 8. Risk apa yang mendapat premium dalam CAPM?
> **Systematic / market risk.**

> [!QUESTION]- 9. Apa arti beta 1.4?
> Return security memiliki market sensitivity sekitar 1.4 times market movement dalam beta sense; bukan berarti return pasti 1.4 kali market setiap period.

> [!QUESTION]- 10. Mengapa negative-beta asset dapat memiliki expected return di bawah risk-free rate?
> Karena memberi hedge/insurance ketika market outcomes buruk.

> [!QUESTION]- 11. Apa CAPM formula?
> \(r_i=r_f+\beta_i(E[R_M]-r_f)\).

> [!QUESTION]- 12. Jika risk aversion naik, mengapa current stock return bisa turun?
> Required risk premium naik → discount rate naik → current price turun.

> [!QUESTION]- 13. Mengapa 86 tahun historical return masih dapat menghasilkan expected-return estimate yang lebar?
> Karena stock-return volatility sangat tinggi relative to number of observations.

> [!QUESTION]- 14. Apa hubungan economic variables dengan systematic risk?
> Common economic shocks memengaruhi banyak securities sekaligus dan therefore membentuk co-movement yang tidak dapat fully diversified away.

> [!QUESTION]- 15. Mengapa positive alpha tidak otomatis berarti free money?
> Bisa mencerminkan model CAPM yang incomplete, omitted risk, implementation costs/frictions, atau true mispricing.

# Section 27 — Synthesis: Satu Cerita Besar

Topik 5.4 dapat dipahami sebagai satu chain:

```text
Investors supply capital
        ↓
They care about time value and risk
        ↓
Historical data show risky broad asset portfolios
often earned higher average returns
        ↓
But diversification removes firm-specific risk
        ↓
So total volatility is not the final priced-risk measure
        ↓
Systematic economic/market exposure remains
        ↓
Beta measures sensitivity to market-wide shocks
        ↓
CAPM converts beta into required expected return
        ↓
Economic news changes cash-flow and risk expectations
        ↓
Prices adjust immediately
        ↓
Realized returns emerge ex post
```

The historical evidence and theoretical model must be kept separate:

> **History tells us what happened. Theory tells us what return investors should require for relevant risk under a model. Neither makes future realized return certain.**
