# CF4 — Stage 11: Perspective Matrix

> [!ABSTRACT] Tujuan
> Stage 11 memformalkan satu prinsip penting yang muncul berulang di CF4:
>
> **economic event yang sama dapat mempunyai meaning yang berbeda tergantung siapa yang melihatnya.**
>
> Perspective yang digunakan:
>
> - **Company / Issuer**
> - **Investor / Shareholder**
> - **Creditor / Lender**
> - **Management**
> - **Analyst / Market**
>
> Matrix ini membantu menghindari jawaban yang secara konsep benar tetapi menggunakan stakeholder yang salah.

## 1. Canonical Perspective Protocol

Sebelum menjawab soal:

```text
1. Apa concept/event?
2. Siapa stakeholder utama?
3. Apa cash-flow right / obligation stakeholder tersebut?
4. Apa benefit?
5. Apa risk / cost?
6. Apakah ada conflict dengan stakeholder lain?
7. Baru pilih interpretation.
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Banyak apparent contradiction di CF4 sebenarnya bukan contradiction:
>
> **the same event is being viewed from different sides of the claim.**

## 3. Core Stakeholder Objectives

| Stakeholder | Primary CF4 Objective | Typical Concern |
|---|---|---|
| Company / Issuer | Raise capital, fund operations/projects, preserve flexibility, maximize firm value | Cost of capital, funding access, obligations, liquidity |
| Shareholder / Investor | Receive risk-adjusted return and value appreciation | Dividends, price, dilution, systematic risk |
| Creditor / Lender | Receive contractual payments with acceptable default/recovery risk | Coverage, leverage, priority, collateral, liquidity |
| Management | Operate firm, allocate resources, preserve control/flexibility | Incentives, compensation, empire building, job security |
| Analyst / Market | Estimate value, risk, sustainability, and quality of reported information | Measurement quality, assumptions, comparability, market expectations |

## 4. Mirror Relationships

### Investor Required Return ↔ Issuer Cost of Capital

```text
Investor:
minimum return demanded
        ↕

Issuer:
cost of using that capital
```

This is the most important mirror relationship in CF4.

Examples:

- equity investor required return ↔ cost of equity;
- debt investor/lender required return ↔ debt financing cost;
- aggregate capital-provider required return ↔ WACC.

### Dividend

```text
Company:
cash leaves firm
        ↕

Shareholder:
cash return received
```

### Interest

```text
Issuer:
financing cost
        ↕

Debt investor:
contractual income
```

### Security Price

```text
Issuer:
external valuation / financing signal
        ↕

Investor:
current market value of claim
```

## 5. Priority / Claim Perspective

For a deteriorating company:

```text
Asset value
→ senior secured creditors
→ other senior debt
→ subordinated debt
→ preferred
→ common equity
```

The exact legal ranking depends on contract/source context, but the canonical reasoning is:

> **more senior claim = more protection before residual claimants receive value.**

Thus:

- a covenant or collateral package can be good for creditors;
- the same package can restrict management/issuer flexibility;
- common shareholders remain residual claimants.

## 6. Financing Perspective Matrix

### More Debt

**Company**
- funding source;
- potential tax shield;
- fixed obligations;
- lower flexibility.

**Shareholder**
- leverage can amplify returns;
- downside/default risk increases.

**Creditor**
- more leverage weakens cushion;
- demands protection/yield.

**Management**
- debt can impose discipline;
- can constrain discretionary spending.

**Analyst**
- tests whether benefit exceeds distress/agency cost.

### More Equity

**Company**
- less fixed-payment pressure;
- potential higher financing cost/dilution.

**Shareholder**
- safer capital structure but ownership dilution possible.

**Creditor**
- stronger loss-absorbing cushion.

**Management**
- greater cash-flow flexibility.

**Analyst**
- evaluates issue price, use of funds, cost of equity.

## 7. Capital-Budgeting Perspective Matrix

### Positive-NPV Project

**Company**
- should increase enterprise value if assumptions hold.

**Shareholder**
- residual value should increase.

**Creditor**
- may benefit from stronger future cash flows, but also assess project risk.

**Management**
- may gain scale/control, creating possible agency overlay.

**Analyst**
- checks cash flows, discount rate, assumptions, externalities.

### High-IRR but Lower-NPV Project

Potential conflict:

```text
Management preference for impressive percentage return
        vs
Shareholder objective of maximum absolute value creation
```

Canonical rule:

> **percentage attractiveness does not override value creation.**

## 8. Payout Perspective Matrix

### Dividend / Repurchase Increase

**Shareholder**
- cash returned sooner.

**Company**
- less cash retained.

**Creditor**
- lower asset/cash cushion.

**Management**
- less discretionary cash; can reduce agency problem but reduce flexibility.

**Analyst**
- asks whether payout is sustainable and whether investment opportunities are being sacrificed.

Thus payout can simultaneously:

- benefit shareholders seeking distributions;
- discipline managers;
- concern creditors;
- harm value if positive-NPV reinvestment is forgone.

## 9. Governance Perspective Matrix

### Agency Relationship

Common CF4 relationships:

```text
Shareholder → Manager
Creditor → Shareholder / Manager
Controlling shareholder → Minority shareholder
```

The same corporate action may transfer wealth across claimants.

Examples:

- risky project after debt issuance can benefit shareholders while harming creditors;
- excessive cash retention can benefit empire-building management while harming shareholders;
- restrictive covenants protect creditors but constrain management.

### Governance Mechanism

Canonical interpretation:

```text
Monitoring / incentives / voting / takeover threat / covenants
→ reduce a specific conflict
→ but impose implementation/flexibility cost
```

Governance is therefore a **trade-off**, not an automatic good.

## 10. Derivative Perspective Matrix

### Hedger

Goal:

> reduce existing exposure.

### Speculator

Goal:

> create directional exposure.

### Option Holder

Has a **right**, not obligation.

### Option Writer

Has the corresponding **obligation** if exercised.

### Forward/Futures Parties

Both sides have contractual exposure.

Therefore:

```text
same underlying move
→ opposite contract payoff
```

but total economic effect depends on whether the contract is hedging another exposure.

## 11. Market / Economic Perspective Matrix

### Interest Rate Increase

**Issuer**
- new borrowing cost may rise.

**Existing fixed-rate bond investor**
- market price falls.

**New lender/investor**
- new yields may be more attractive.

**Equity investor**
- discount rate rises, but operating cash-flow outlook may also change.

**Analyst**
- separates cash-flow and discount-rate channels.

### Inflation Increase

**Issuer**
- input costs and nominal financing costs may rise; pricing power matters.

**Investor**
- requires purchasing-power compensation.

**Fixed nominal creditor**
- real value of fixed payments can erode.

**Analyst**
- converts nominal to real and evaluates cash-flow pass-through.

## 12. Perspective Traps

1. **“Higher interest is good.”**
   - Good for new lender income;
   - bad for issuer financing cost;
   - bad for existing fixed-rate bond price.

2. **“Higher dividend is good.”**
   - Good current cash return for shareholder;
   - but reduces company liquidity and creditor cushion.

3. **“More debt is good because tax shield.”**
   - Ignores creditor/default risk and shareholder downside.

4. **“Secured debt is safer.”**
   - Safer for protected creditor;
   - more restrictive for issuer and junior claimants.

5. **“Hedge lost money, therefore hedge failed.”**
   - A hedge may lose on derivative while underlying exposure gains.
   - Evaluate combined position.

6. **“Positive realized investor return means company created accounting profit.”**
   - Market return and accounting profit are different quantities.

7. **“Good governance benefits everyone equally.”**
   - Mechanisms can impose costs and redistribute control/flexibility.
